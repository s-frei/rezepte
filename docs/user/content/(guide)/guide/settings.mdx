## Profile<NewIn release="v1.4.0" />

The page opens with one card: how Rezepte shows you to the household at the top, then your account, then how you use the app.

### You: photo, name and color

The band across the top is your color, with your initial set into it, and your photo sits on its edge — or, without one, your initial in your color. Under it are your display name and a line with your role and the login name you sign in with. The login name is shown, not editable, and it stays the same however often you rename yourself.

The camera on the photo adds one: it opens a file picker for a JPEG, PNG or WebP of up to 10 MiB. Once you have a photo, the camera opens a small menu with "Change photo" and "Remove photo". A new photo appears in a round frame to place: drag it to move, use the slider, a pinch or the mouse wheel to zoom in to four times, or use the arrow keys to move and plus and minus to zoom. Confirm, and the round section you framed becomes your photo, here and wherever Rezepte shows you — on the recipes you added, in the list of people and in your person card (see [Tour of the app](/guide#person-cards)). The photo is cut from the file you chose, so to frame it differently, choose the file again. A photo, or a framed section, smaller than about 64 pixels is refused with a message. "Remove photo" puts your initial back.

<Screenshot name="avatar-crop" caption="Framing a photo: the round window shows what the circles will show" />

If a photo is missing or cannot be loaded, Rezepte shows your initial in your color instead. Your color and initial still identify you, so nobody has to recognize a face to know whose recipe it is.

The pencil beside your name turns it into a field with "Save" and "Cancel". The display name is the name on your recipes, in the list of people and on the circles that sign a recipe card: up to 64 characters, and saving it empty puts your login name back. It does not have to be unique, so two people can both be "Mia".

The eight circles under your name are the colors to choose from, and yours marks you wherever the app names you. Tapping one saves it right away, no button, and the band follows. The line under them says who else wears the chosen color — "Sage is also worn by Sofia, Ida and Tom" — or that nobody else does. Sharing a color is fine: your initial stays in the circle either way.

### Your account

**Email** shows your address, or "none". It is optional and never used to sign in. It is where Rezepte sends a link when you forget your password, but only once it is confirmed. When you type a new address under **Change** and mail is set up, the window says before you save that a confirmation mail goes to it, and the button reads **Save and send mail**; the same address in other capital letters is the same mailbox and sends nothing. After saving, a badge beside **Email** says **unconfirmed**, and a box under the address says **Mail sent**, with a bar from the day it went out to the day its link stops working (7 days later) and **Send again**. **Send again** works once a minute, and saving the address counts as the first send. If the link ran out before you opened it, the badge says **expired**, the bar shows the day it ran out, and the box offers **Send new**; if no mail went out, it says **No mail sent** with **Send**. Once you open the link the badge says **confirmed**. A link that was used already or has expired says so, with **Go to your profile** to come back here and send a new one, or **Sign in** first when you are signed out. An address your identity provider vouched for counts as confirmed. To remove the address, clear the field and save.

<Screenshot name="profile-email-unconfirmed" caption="An address waiting for its confirmation" />

**Password** opens a small dialog: enter the current password once, then the new password (8–128 characters) twice. Changing your password signs out every other device that was logged in as you; this device stays signed in. An account without a password yet says so, and the button reads **Set**.

While you type the new password, a bar below it rates it from "very weak" to "strong". It knows the most common leaked passwords, keyboard runs like `qwertz` and your own names, so `anna1990` counts as weak for Anna. The rating is advice: any password of the right length can be saved. A password manager's generated password is the easy way to "strong".

**Sign-in** is there only when your instance offers sign-in through an identity provider such as Google (the owner sets that up under Sign-in below, see [Single sign-on](/operations/single-sign-on)). **Connect** takes you to the provider and back; from then on the provider's button on the login page signs you in without a password. Once connected, the row says since when, and **Disconnect** undoes it and signs you out on your other devices — but only while your account also has a password, because without one you could no longer sign in at all. The row says so; set a password first.

<Screenshot name="settings-signin" caption="The sign-in row, before the account is connected" />

### How you use Rezepte

**Language** is "English" or "German", and picking one reloads the page into it. It is saved to your account rather than to this browser, so it follows you to any device you sign in from. It changes the interface only; it never translates a recipe's own title, ingredients or steps. Missing your language? See [Add a language](/contributing/add-a-language).

**Appearance** is "System", "Light" or "Dark"; "System" follows your device. The same choice sits in the account menu behind your initial at the top right, and on a phone in the "You" sheet. Unlike your language, the theme is stored in this browser only — it does not follow you to another device or browser.

## Notifications

Under **Settings → Notifications** you choose what Rezepte tells you by mail, which arrives only once your email address is confirmed, and as a push notification to the devices you enable there, with a test for the device in your hand. The owner also sets there the hour the daily new recipes go out. See [Notifications](/guide/notifications).

## App

The "Rezepte as an app" card offers to install Rezepte on this device, where your browser can: a button on Android and in Chrome or Edge on a computer, the three Safari steps on an iPhone or iPad. The card is hidden once the app is installed, except on an iPhone, where Safari cannot tell and the card stays in Safari. [Install Rezepte as an app](/guide/install-app) covers it all, including why the owner sees a note here while the instance runs on plain HTTP.

## Help

The "Help & guide" card opens this guide in a new tab. It is the way here from inside the app whenever something is unclear.

## About Rezepte

The last card on the page says which version of Rezepte your instance runs, and links to its source code and to the [Changelog](/changelog) — handy when you ask for help or wonder what an update brought.

## API

<Screenshot name="api" caption="The API tab, here as an admin sees it" />

Every signed-in member has this tab. Its "Access for programs" card reads like a cookbook page with two recipes, each with its ingredients and its steps:

- **For AI assistants** — the MCP address an assistant such as Claude Code connects to, and an API token. Step one points to the "API tokens" card where you create the token (or, if you are not an admin, says who can issue one); "Setup guide" opens [Connect an AI assistant](/api/mcp).
- **For your own scripts** — the base URL of this instance's API, and either a token or your username and password: a script may also sign in the way the app does. "Interactive docs" opens the try-it-out documentation, "openapi.json" the raw document — both work straight from the browser with your session, no token needed. See [API](/api) for what you can do with it.

Both addresses have a "Copy" button. Admins find the "API tokens" card below, with "Create token" and their own tokens — or, with "Everyone in the household" switched on, every admin's. Once they hold a valid token, step one is marked done. [API tokens](/api/tokens) covers it.

## Shared links

Every signed-in member has a "Shared links" tab: the public links you have created, across every recipe, with their status and Copy and Revoke for each — see [Public links](/guide/sharing#public-links). You can narrow the list by status. Admins can also switch to everyone's links there, narrow them to one person, and revoke them all.

## Email<Audience role="owner" />

The owner has an "Email" tab under "Household", between "People" and "Import & export", for the mail server Rezepte sends its mail through: setup links, links for a new password, address confirmations, security notices and notifications — see [Mail](/guide/mail).

## Sign-in page<Audience role="owner" />

The owner has a "Sign-in" tab, next to "Email", for the identity provider people may sign in with instead of a password: Google, or one you run yourself. The card shows the provider and the client, whether sign-in is on, and the redirect URI to register at the provider. "Set up sign-in" and "Edit" open the window for the issuer address, client ID, secret and button name; "Turn off" removes them. When the settings come from environment variables, the stamp reads "Set by the server" and nothing can be changed here. [Single sign-on](/operations/single-sign-on) walks through it.

## Import and export<Audience role="admin" />

Admins have one more tab, "Import & export", for passing recipes with their photos to another Rezepte — see [Import and export](/guide/import-export).

## Signing out

"Sign out" ends the current session on this device. You find it in the "Account menu" (your initial, top right on desktop) and at the foot of the Settings sidebar; on a phone, in the "You" sheet of the bottom bar and at the foot of the Settings "Contents" sheet.

The instance owner can change another person's display name, color and photo on that person's page under "People"; nobody else can. See [People](/guide/users) for that and the rest of the account management admins do there. The owner also finds the household's **Recipe editing** setting there (see [Editing rights](/guide/editing-rights)), **Link previews** and **Public sharing** (see [Sharing recipes](/guide/sharing)).
