title: Tasty
description: A mark one member leaves on somebody else's recipe, counted and named for everyone, stored like a favorite
---

A favorite is for the person who sets it; a tasty mark is for the person who wrote the recipe. A member who cooked something and liked it marks it tasty, and from then on everyone in the household sees how many members found it tasty and who. The UI calls it "Lecker" in German. [Data model](/architecture/data-model) owns the table; [HTTP API](/architecture/api) owns the endpoints.

## How it works

**One row per (user, recipe), the shape of favorites.** A mark is a row in `tasty`, declared in `service/internal/db/migrations/0003_tasty.sql`: marking inserts it with `INSERT OR IGNORE`, clearing deletes it, both foreign keys cascade and `tasty_recipe_idx` serves the per-recipe counts. `SetTasty` in `service/internal/recipe/tasty.go` follows `SetFavorite` line for line - a state rather than an event, `ErrNotFound` when marking a missing recipe, clearing never checked - and [Favorites](/features/favorites) explains each of those choices once.

**The author cannot mark their own recipe.** `SetTasty` reads the recipe row it already fetches for the existence check and returns `ErrOwnRecipe` when `created_by` is the caller; `set-tasty` answers `403` and MCP's `add_tasty` a tool error that says why. Clearing is not checked, since the author never has a mark to clear. The rule keys on `created_by` alone: somebody who only edited a recipe may still mark it. Deleting a user is the one other way to become an author: `user.Service.Delete` hands the deleted user's recipes to the acting admin, so `DeleteTastyOfNewOwner` drops the admin's own marks on exactly those recipes first, in the same transaction - kept, they would count for recipes the admin now owns, with no button to take them back.

**The caller comes from the auth context and from nowhere else**, as for favorites: `set-tasty` and `delete-tasty` (`/api/v1/recipes/{id}/tasty`) read `auth.UserFrom(ctx)`, and MCP's `add_tasty` and `remove_tasty` act as the token owner (see [MCP](/features/mcp)).

**The count and the names are public, the caller's own mark is not.** `Recipe.tastyCount` and `Recipe.tastyBy` (people, in the order they marked) are the same for every reader, so `load` fills them from `ListTastyUsers` and `ByID`/`BySlug` return them; `Recipe.tasty`, whether the caller is among them, is filled by `fillCaller` beside `favorite`. `Card` carries `tastyCount` and `tasty`: `toCards` runs `ListTastyCounts` once per page for every caller and `ListTastyRecipeIDs` once more when there is a caller, beside the favorite and tag batches, so a page stays at a fixed number of queries.

**Tastiest is an order, not a filter.** `sort=tasty` orders by `(SELECT COUNT(*) FROM tasty t WHERE t.recipe_id = r.id) DESC`, then by the default order, `updated_at DESC, id DESC` - one more entry in `recipeOrderBy` in `service/internal/db/sqlc/recipe_list.go` ([Recipes and search](/features/recipes) owns how sorting is built). Recipes nobody marked still list, last.

**The heart mirrors the star.** `TastyButton.svelte` is a pill with a heart and the count, optimistic with rollback and a toast like `FavoriteStar.svelte`, moving the count with the heart; its `readonly` mode is a pill with nothing to press, and `fluid` a read-only pill that sizes itself to the card it sits on and reads as a caption on the photo rather than a button: the lightbox's near-black at 60%, the same in both themes, over a blurred patch and without a shadow, so a phone card offers nothing that looks pressable. A display grows with its card and a button keeps the size a finger needs: the fluid pill runs from 24px on a 150px card to 36px from a 220px card up, linearly in between, so the count never shrinks while the card grows - also not where the phone layout turns into the desktop one, where a 357px phone card becomes a 220px desktop card. The button is 44px on a phone, where the recipe page is the only place to set the mark, and 36px from `md` up, like the star. On a card it sits in the bottom-left corner of the image, where the star sits top right: from `md` up it is the button, painted over the card's stretched link because it comes later in the document; below `md`, and on the author's own recipe everywhere, it is the read-only pill, shown only once somebody marked the recipe, with `pointer-events-none` so a tap falls through to the link. Which of the two a card shows follows the layout (`md`), never the card's width, and the grid keeps a desktop card at 220px or wider so the button and the star leave the photo visible (see [Favorites](/features/favorites)). The recipe page sets the button beside the star in the line under the title (`RecipeView`'s `byline` snippet), followed by "Tasty for" and `TastyPeople.svelte` - initials and a popover with the names, the same popover pattern as the author initials: the count and the people it counts read as one line, and the title keeps the whole width, where beside it the pair would leave it about 180px on a phone and break even "Königsberger Klopse" into three lines. After a toggle the page adds or removes the reader's own circle from `tastyBy` locally rather than reloading the recipe.

**The corner is computed from the card's width.** The image is square and as wide as the card, so its bottom edge is one card width below the card's top. The card is a size container (`@container`), the pill's `top` is `100cqw` minus the padding and the 6px inset, and `-translate-y-full` hangs the pill above that line whatever height it has - `RecipeCard.svelte` spells out the numbers. Anchoring to the bottom of the card instead would move with the title's height and the tag line.

## Why it is this way

Feedback to the author is the point, so the author marking their own recipe would only inflate the number everyone reads. Showing who, not only how many, is what makes the mark worth leaving in a household: the author knows the people behind it.

On a phone the card image is about 150px wide, and a 36px button over it covers a large part of the photo; a card is also mostly tapped to open the recipe, where a stray tap on a button costs a toggle. So phone cards show the count and the recipe page takes the input - the same reason the star is off phone cards (see [Favorites](/features/favorites)).

Counting in a subquery rather than keeping a counter column is cheap at a household's scale and leaves one source of truth: a counter on `recipes` would be one more value every mark, clear, and user deletion has to keep in step.

## Rejected

- **Naming it "like".** It reads as social-media approval of anything; "tasty" (Lecker) says what the mark is about, a dish somebody cooked.
- **A `tasty_count` column on `recipes`.** Faster to sort on, but it duplicates what the rows already say and drifts the moment a cascade deletes rows without touching it.
- **Letting the author mark their own recipe.** See above; it turns feedback into a number anyone can raise.
- **A toggle on phone cards.** It covers the photo and invites stray taps; the recipe page has the room.

## Limits

- Like favorites, marks disappear with the user who left them (`ON DELETE CASCADE`), so deleting a member lowers counts.
- There is no notification: the author sees a mark when they next look at the recipe or the overview.
- There is no filter by tasty, only the order.
- `tastyBy` lists everyone who marked a recipe, unpaginated; a household has too few members for that to matter.
