title: Tasty
description: A mark one member leaves on somebody else's recipe, counted and named for everyone, stored like a favorite, and told to the author a minute later
---

A favorite is for the person who sets it; a tasty mark is for the person who wrote the recipe. A member who cooked something and liked it marks it tasty, and from then on everyone in the household sees how many members found it tasty and who. The UI calls it "Lecker" in German. [Data model](/architecture/data-model) owns the table; [HTTP API](/architecture/api) owns the endpoints.

## How it works

**One row per (user, recipe), the shape of favorites.** A mark is a row in `tasty`, declared in `service/internal/db/migrations/0003_tasty.sql`: marking inserts it with `INSERT OR IGNORE` (`:execrows`, so `SetTasty` knows whether the mark is new), clearing deletes it, both foreign keys cascade and `tasty_recipe_idx` serves the per-recipe counts. `SetTasty` in `service/internal/recipe/tasty.go` follows `SetFavorite` line for line - a state rather than an event, `ErrNotFound` when marking a missing recipe, clearing never checked - and [Favorites](/features/favorites) explains each of those choices once.

**The author cannot mark their own recipe.** `SetTasty` reads the recipe row it already fetches for the existence check and returns `ErrOwnRecipe` when `created_by` is the caller; `set-tasty` answers `403` and MCP's `add_tasty` a tool error that says why. Clearing is not checked, since the author never has a mark to clear. The rule keys on `created_by` alone: somebody who only edited a recipe may still mark it. Deleting a user is the one other way to become an author: `user.Service.Delete` hands the deleted user's recipes to the acting admin, so `DeleteTastyOfNewOwner` drops the admin's own marks on exactly those recipes first, in the same transaction - kept, they would count for recipes the admin now owns, with no button to take them back.

**The caller comes from the auth context and from nowhere else**, as for favorites: `set-tasty` and `delete-tasty` (`/api/v1/recipes/{id}/tasty`) read `auth.UserFrom(ctx)`, and MCP's `add_tasty` and `remove_tasty` act as the token owner (see [MCP](/features/mcp)).

**The count and the names are public, the caller's own mark is not.** `Recipe.tastyCount` and `Recipe.tastyBy` (people, in the order they marked) are the same for every reader, so `load` fills them from `ListTastyUsers` and `ByID`/`BySlug` return them; `Recipe.tasty`, whether the caller is among them, is filled by `fillCaller` beside `favorite`. `Card` carries `tastyCount` and `tasty`: `toCards` runs `ListTastyCounts` once per page for every caller and `ListTastyRecipeIDs` once more when there is a caller, beside the favorite and tag batches, so a page stays at a fixed number of queries.

**Tastiest is an order, not a filter.** `sort=tasty` orders by `(SELECT COUNT(*) FROM tasty t WHERE t.recipe_id = r.id) DESC`, then by the default order, `updated_at DESC, id DESC` - one more entry in `recipeOrderBy` in `service/internal/db/sqlc/recipe_list.go` ([Recipes and search](/features/recipes) owns how sorting is built). Recipes nobody marked still list, last.

**The author hears of a new mark, a minute later.** A mark that did not exist before goes to the `TastyNotifier`, which is `notify.Service`; a minute later, if the mark is still there, the recipe's `created_by` gets a mail (headed with the title and "went down well", the marker's picture, a button to the recipe) and a push (the marker "finds your recipe … tasty"), each unless switched off for that channel. A mark taken back within the minute, or set again, tells nothing more. [Notifications](/features/notifications) owns the rules and the push; [Email](/features/email) the sender and the templates.

**The heart mirrors the star.** `TastyButton.svelte` is optimistic with rollback and a toast like `FavoriteStar.svelte`, moving the count with the heart. Its `readonly` mode has nothing to press, and `accent` paints the read-only count in the accent's sand instead of the light surface.

- On a card from `md` up it is the button in the image's bottom-left corner, opposite the star, painted over the card's stretched link because it comes later in the document; on the author's own recipe it is the read-only pill there, with `pointer-events-none` so a tap falls through to the link. Below `md` it is the read-only pill in the footer (see below), shown only once somebody marked the recipe, and not positioned, so a tap reaches the link. Which of the two a card shows follows the layout (`md`), never the card's width, and the grid keeps a desktop card at 220px or wider so the button and the star leave the photo visible (see [Favorites](/features/favorites)).
- On the recipe page the button sits beside the star in the line under the title (`RecipeView`'s `byline` snippet), followed by `TastyPeople.svelte`: the faces and one popover listing every marking person, the author initials' pattern. One popover for the row rather than one per face, because the row shows at most four faces and "+N", and a per-face popover could not reach the people behind the count. The line under the title holds them so the title keeps the whole width; beside it the pair would break even "Königsberger Klopse" into three lines on a phone.
- After a toggle the page adds or removes the reader's own circle from `tastyBy` locally rather than reloading the recipe, and names it `changed` (a writable `$derived` on the recipe, so another recipe starts without one): only that circle moves. It pops in with `animate-pop` (from 0.6, 200ms on `--ease-spring`, 240ms late so it lands after the heart's first beat; `backwards` fill, so a finished pop leaves no animation behind), the others make room with `animate:flip`, and a removed one fades in 120ms. When the tap starts or empties the stack, "Tasty by" fades with the circle, since the whole line comes and goes. Reduced motion shows the change at once.

**The corner is anchored to the photo box.** The photo keeps its own ratio (4:5 to 16:9), so its bottom edge is not a fixed distance below the card's top; the pill sits in a wrapper that is exactly the photo's box, in its bottom-left corner. From `md` up the heart on a photo shares the center of the photo's bottom-left corner like the star its top-right one: 28px in the 20px corner, 6px in ([Design system](/architecture/design-system) states the rule). A card without a photo has no corner to anchor to: there the button moves into the footer, before the author initials, as tall as them with their 2px ring (32px beside 28px circles).

**On a phone the count sits in the card's bottom-left corner.** A phone card's footer gives the tags a line of their own and puts the count and the initials on the line below, the count's end cap on the center of the card's 24px corner as the initials on the other one's. It is 28px, as tall as the 24px initials with their ring, on photo and text cards alike: in the accent's sand on a photo card, whose light surface would swallow a light pill in dark mode, light on a text card's tint. Taking the count off the photo is what leaves the photo's top-right corner to the [star](/features/favorites).

## Why it is this way

Feedback to the author is the point, so the author marking their own recipe would only inflate the number everyone reads. Showing who, not only how many, is what makes the mark worth leaving in a household: the author knows the people behind it. The minute before telling the author is for a mis-tap: a heart pressed and taken back should not reach anybody's phone.

On a phone the card image is about 150px wide, and a 36px button over it covers a large part of the photo; a card is also mostly tapped to open the recipe, where a stray tap on a button costs a toggle. So phone cards show the count and the recipe page takes the input.

Counting in a subquery rather than keeping a counter column is cheap at a household's scale and leaves one source of truth: a counter on `recipes` would be one more value every mark, clear, and user deletion has to keep in step.

## Rejected

- **Naming it "like".** It reads as social-media approval of anything; "tasty" (Lecker) says what the mark is about, a dish somebody cooked.
- **A `tasty_count` column on `recipes`.** Faster to sort on, but it duplicates what the rows already say and drifts the moment a cascade deletes rows without touching it.
- **Letting the author mark their own recipe.** See above; it turns feedback into a number anyone can raise.
- **A toggle on phone cards.** It covers the photo and invites stray taps; the recipe page has the room.

## Limits

- Like favorites, marks disappear with the user who left them (`ON DELETE CASCADE`), so deleting a member lowers counts.
- A notification waits in memory for its minute: a restart in that minute drops it, and the author sees the mark only when they next look.
- Only a mark taken back within its minute is silent: a member who marks, clears and marks again more than a minute apart tells the author each time. Members are trusted, so nothing remembers past marks to stop that.
- There is no filter by tasty, only the order.
- `tastyBy` lists everyone who marked a recipe, unpaginated; a household has too few members for that to matter.
