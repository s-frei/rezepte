---
title: Shopping lists
description: Lists filled from recipes and by hand, sorted by department, shared by invitation with other people on the instance, with server-side scaling and instance-wide department overrides
---

A signed-in person keeps any number of shopping lists, alone or shared with others on the instance, fills them from recipes (the basket on a recipe's ingredient stub) and by typing, ticks items off and sees them either by store department or by recipe. The service side is `service/internal/shopping` (pure rules, service, huma handler); the frontend is `frontend/src/routes/lists/`, `frontend/src/lib/components/shopping/`, `frontend/src/lib/shopping/` and the client `frontend/src/lib/api/shopping.ts`.

## How it works

**Access is the creator or an accepted member.** Every query is scoped by one predicate: `created_by = me` or an accepted row in `shopping_list_members` for me. Anyone else, a pending invitee included, gets exactly the answer an id that does not exist gets: `shopping.ErrNotFound`, HTTP 404, never 403, on reads and on every change, so a guessed id tells nobody that the list exists (`TestForeignShoppingListAnswers404`, `TestPendingInviteeCannotRead`). Admins have no extra reach. Deleting an account deletes its lists and its memberships. The tables and their delete rules are on [Data model](/architecture/data-model#shopping-lists).

**Adding a recipe copies it.** `AddRecipe` writes one entry (title, the recipe's servings as `base_servings`, the chosen `servings`) and one item per picked ingredient, picked by `(group, index)` into the recipe as it is at that moment. The items keep name, unscaled quantity, unit, note and group name. Editing the recipe afterwards never reaches the list (`TestRecipeEditDoesNotReachList`); deleting it nulls `recipe_id` and the list keeps the entry's title without a link (`TestDeletedRecipeKeepsItems`). Adding the same recipe twice makes two entries. An unknown recipe is `ErrRecipeNotFound` (404 "recipe not found"), an empty pick list `ErrInvalid` (422).

**Quantities scale on the server, on every read.** `get` scales an entry's items from `base_servings` to `servings` with `ScaleQuantity` and rounds with `RoundScaled` in `service/internal/shopping/scale.go`, a port of `frontend/src/lib/recipe/scale.ts` (thirds and quarters below ten, whole numbers from ten). `TestRoundScaledMatchesFrontend` carries the rows of `frontend/src/lib/recipe/scale.test.ts`: a change to rounding on one side changes both files and both tables. Changing an entry's servings is one column; its items rescale, ticked ones too (a tick stays a tick), and an item without a quantity stays without one (`TestSetServingsRescalesAndKeepsTicks`).

**Typed text splits into amount, unit, name and note.** `ParseFree` in `freetext.go` reads "2 l Milch" or "500g Mehl" as amount, unit and name, with the units the recipe editor suggests in both languages; text without a leading amount is all name. The first ", ", " – " or " - " after the name (`noteSepRe`), or a closing "(…)", starts the note. The list page's add card has the recipe editor's amount and unit fields (`AddItemCard`, reusing `UnitInput`); `add-shopping-item` takes optional `quantity` and `unit` that replace what `ParseFree` found in `text` (`Service.AddItem`), so typing "2 l Milch" into the name alone still works, as it does in shopping mode's single field and over MCP. The added item's row flashes where it landed, without scrolling (several items are typed in a row) (`flash` on `ShoppingRow`, `animate-flash`), and a toast names its department. The add-to-list dialog says when the recipe is on the picked list already (`add_to_list_already`, from `recipeId` on each summary's recipes), since adding it again adds its amounts to the same lines. On a shared list the faces in the header open `PeopleSheet` (`PeopleList`, the side card's "Shared with"), so phones and tablets, which have no side column, show who is on the list too. A free item remembers who typed it (`added_by`, shown as "by …"): `Source.addedBy` keeps the whole `ListPerson`, and `addedByName` is the first name, the full name when two people on the list (owner, members and adders) share it (`holderNames` in `#lib/user/holders.ts`). On an open row someone else's item shows their `PersonMark` `xs` before "by Jonas" (the mark `aria-hidden`, the words carry the meaning); the viewer's own stay neutral ("by you"), and a done row names the adder in words only, since its end already holds the ticker's face - never two faces in one row. Every item remembers who ticked it (`checked_by`, set and cleared with `checked_at` by `SetChecked`, returned as `checkedBy` only while ticked); on a shared list a done row ends in that person's mark and first name ("You" for your own), several people's marks stacked without names (`Row.checkedBy`, `ShoppingRow`'s `checkers`).

**Names meet through a key.** `NameKey` lowercases, trims, collapses spaces, folds French and Spanish accents (é, ç, ñ…, never ä, ö, ü, ß) and strips a German or English plural ending from the last word, with `irregularPlurals` for what the suffix rules cannot reach ("Äpfel", "Eier"). "Zwiebel", "Zwiebeln" and " zwiebeln " share a key. The key is stored with the item when it is written.

**A department comes from an override or the word list.** `shopping_department_overrides` wins; otherwise `DefaultDepartment` looks the key up in `words` in `departments.go`, one German and English word list per department, exact match first, then the longest known word the key ends with ("rinderhackfleisch" ends with "hackfleisch"), which needs four letters or a word boundary so "graham" never lands on "ham". A qualifier after a comma or in parentheses is cut off for the lookup only ("Rinderhackfleisch, gemischt (halb und halb)" is meat; the key keeps it). Unknown names go to `other`. The order of `Departments` is the aisle order on the page. Recipes carry no language, so every name is looked up in both languages at once. `TestDemoIngredientsAreSorted` puts every ingredient of both demo sample sets through it and fails on any that lands in `other` without a reason in `demoOther`, which is empty: every demo ingredient has a department. The sample recipes stand for real ones, and a common ingredient in `other` defeats the sorting.

**Moving an item teaches the whole instance.** The item sheet's department choice calls `PUT /api/v1/shopping-departments` with the row's name; `SetDepartment` upserts the override for its key, and every list of every person reads it on its next load (`TestDepartmentOverrideAppliesToAllLists`).

**The API takes a session or a token and every change answers with the whole list.** The routes are in `handler.go` under the `shopping-lists` tag; the three readers (list, get, events) need `lists:read`, every other operation `lists:write` (`auth.Protected`), and a token acts as its issuer, so it sees only the issuer's lists (`TestTokenActsAsIssuer`). The operations: list, create, get, rename and delete lists; add a recipe, change an entry's servings, remove an entry; add a free item, tick or untick and remove items by id, clear the ticked ones; set a department. Each change runs in one transaction that first touches the list (which proves ownership and moves `updated_at`) and returns the list as it is afterwards, scaled and sorted, so the client replaces its copy instead of patching it.

**Every committed change is announced over a stream.** `GET /api/v1/shopping-lists/{id}/events` (`stream.go`, `lists:read`, 404 like every other read) answers `text/event-stream`: `retry: 3000` first, then `event: changed` / `data: {"revision":N}` per change and a `: ping` comment every 25 s (`pingEvery`, shortened in tests through `export_test.go`). `revision` (column `shopping_lists.revision`, also on every list answer) counts the list's changes: `TouchShoppingList` and `RenameShoppingList` raise it in the change's own statement, `Service.changed` raises it for member changes and `SetDepartment` for every list (`TestRevisionCountsEveryChange`). The stream reads it when it sends, so the event names the list as it is then; a page that shows that revision already - its own change, whose answer carried it - skips the refetch, every other page refetches. A timestamp would not do: `updated_at` has whole seconds, and two people ticking in the same second would share it. Behind it sits `Hub` (`hub.go`): one buffered channel of size one per open stream, so a burst of changes coalesces into one signal and a publisher never blocks. The service publishes after the transaction committed - every `mutate`, `Rename`, `Delete`, `Invite` that inserted a row, `RemoveMember`, `Accept`, `Leave` - and `SetDepartment` signals every open stream, since an override reaches every list (`TestChangesPublish`). After each signal the stream reads the revision through `GetShoppingListRevision`, which finds no row without access, so a removed member's stream ends (`TestStreamEndsWhenAccessLost`). Every `Service` makes its own hub; `main.go` registers `Service.Hub().Close` with `httpserver.Server.OnShutdown`, which ends every stream when shutdown starts instead of letting `Shutdown` wait for them. Stream tests need a real `httptest.Server`; a `ResponseRecorder` neither flushes nor streams. A reverse proxy must not buffer the path: the reverse-proxy page of the user docs says how, and an open list holds one HTTP/1.1 connection of the browser's six per host.

**The client follows the stream and refetches.** `followList` (`frontend/src/lib/shopping/live.ts`) opens an `EventSource` per list id with credentials and calls `onchange` for each `changed` event, coalescing a burst within 150 ms into one call with the burst's highest revision (none once an `open` or an error joined it; `ListState.follow` refetches unless it shows that revision), and again on every `open`, the first included: a change between the page's own fetch and the subscription, or during a reconnect, sends no event. When the server ends the stream for good the reconnect gets a 404 and the source closes; `onchange` then fires once, so the refetch meets the same 404 and the page shows `list_gone`. `ListState` (`list-state.svelte.ts`) owns the list, the revision counter described below, `toggle` and `refresh`; `refresh` lets what another device ticked linger: those items sit in `lingering` for `LINGER_MS` (1.5 s), `shown` counts them as open so their row stays in place, and `ShoppingRow` shows such a row ticked and lit before it moves to Done; `refresh` passes only a 404 or a lost session to the page's `onfail` (`failList` in `fail.ts`: toast, and `/lists` on a 404), any other failure passes quietly since the next event or reconnect retries; the list page and the shopping-mode page both use it and subscribe by list id (`followList(listId, …)` in an `$effect`), so linking from one list to the next re-subscribes.

**Shopping mode is its own route.** `/lists/[id]/shop` is bare like cook mode (no app bars), reached from the list's "Shop" button (`shopHref`): on a phone a wide button under the title that carries the counts (a description through `aria-describedby`, so its name stays "Shop") and a progress line along its foot, from a tablet on the first of the header's buttons. It holds the screen awake with `ScreenWakeLock` the way the cook page does (acquire on mount, again on `visibilitychange`, release on teardown) and shows the caption only while the lock is real. Its head is a three-column grid - close, the centered name, faces - and the faces say under whose account the ticks go, since the page has no top bar or dock to show it: the viewer's `PersonMark sm` at the end, `ringed` like the top bar's own avatar so it reads as the viewer's beside the others (named "Checking off as …"), and on a shared list a `FaceStack` of everyone else before it. Items stay neutral; nothing in a row takes a person's color. Rules: each recipe's amount on its own line (`ShoppingRow` with `shop`), the whole row ticks on a tap, done rows sit collapsed under a "done" row with its count, the recipe view hides recipe groups whose rows are all ticked and has no entry menu, and there is no add field (owner's call: the list is kept before, the store is for ticking); the view switch spans the column on every screen (`ViewSwitch full`); the rows fade out at both ends of their scroll area (a `mask-image` gradient, with padding so a row at either end sits clear of the fade) instead of meeting the top and the screen's foot at a hard edge.

**Sharing is an invitation the invitee answers.** `Invite(userID, listID, inviteeID)` is the creator's alone, refuses oneself and an unknown account (`ErrInvalid`), and inserts a `shopping_list_members` row without `accepted_at`; inviting someone who already has a row (pending or accepted) changes nothing and notifies nobody (`TestInviteIsIdempotent`). `Accept` sets `accepted_at` on the caller's own pending row, 404 without one. `Leave` deletes the caller's row, accepted or pending, so declining and leaving are one call; the creator cannot leave (`TestCreatorCannotLeave`). `RemoveMember` is the creator withdrawing an invitation or removing a member. Endpoints: `POST /shopping-lists/{id}/invitations {userId}`, `DELETE …/members/{userId}`, `POST …/membership` (accept) and `DELETE …/membership` (leave or decline). The overview answers `{items, invitations}`; an invitation carries only the list's name, the inviter and the open count, nothing of its content. A `List` names `owner`, `members` and, for the creator only, `pending`; a `Summary` adds `updatedBy`, the person who changed the list last (`shopping_lists.updated_by`, null once that account is gone).

**Members do everything but share and delete.** Add and remove recipes and items, tick, rename, change servings: all of it runs under the access predicate. Sharing, removing members and deleting the list check `created_by` instead (`ownList`); a member who tries gets 404, as a stranger does.

**The invitation notifies at once.** `shopping.Service.SetInviteNotifier` takes the `notify` service, called only when the insert added a row, so a repeated invite sends nothing. The kind is `list_invite` ([Notifications](/features/notifications)). The demo seed sets no notifier, so seeding invitations mails nobody.

**A gone list leaves the open page within seconds.** When a person is removed (or the list deleted) while the page is open, the server ends their stream, the reconnect gets 404 and the refetch meets the same 404: the page shows the toast `list_gone` and goes to `/lists` within about 3 s (`failList`). A change sent before that gets the same 404 and the same answer. An invitation withdrawn (or its list deleted) while the overview is open answers Accept or Decline with 404: the card drops out, the dot count follows, and the toast is `list_gone`. A free item whose adder's account is gone shows "from a former member" (`list_added_by_former`) in place of the name.

**The sharing UI.** The overview shows an invitation card per pending invitation (Accept, Decline) above two sections, "Your lists" and "Shared with you"; a card's footer reads "Only you", "Shared with …", "From … · with …" and `Changed by {name} {when}`. An invitation puts a dot on the lists link and slot, from `frontend/src/lib/shopping/invitations.svelte.ts`, which the overview and the shell keep in step with the count; its `label` is the screen-reader text, singular for one. A failed invite toasts `list_share_error`, a failed load of the people picker `users_load_error`. On the list page the owner has "Share" (`ShareDialog`: a picker of people not yet on the list, "Has access" with the owner, members and pending invitees, × to remove) and a menu with Delete; a member has no Share, and the menu offers "Leave list" with a confirmation. `PeopleStack` shows everyone with access in the header, and the side panel's "Shared with" lists them.

**The list page groups on the client.** `frontend/src/lib/shopping/rows.ts` builds the views from the list the server sent:

- **By department**, items with the same `nameKey` form one row with one circle; each source - one per recipe, or per person who typed it in, and unit, so a recipe naming an ingredient in two groups or added twice reads as one amount - shows its amount and recipe on a line of its own, and with more than one source the row's `total` (amounts added up per unit: weights and volumes across units through `frontend/src/lib/recipe/measures.ts`, read in g or kg and ml or l as the sum suits, pieces rounded up since nobody buys a quarter egg, other units only when spelled alike ignoring case; `measures.ts` knows metric units only and is the table a later unit conversion of recipes builds on) stands beside the name and starts its line in the copied text (`rowsOf`, `sumByUnit`, `unitKey` in `rows.ts`). An open row adds up only its open items, and a source ticked by recipe is struck through; an item without an amount never joins one with ("Eier" and "2 Eier" stay two lines) and leaves the row without a total, so it cannot hide in a sum. A row lingers (`Row.lingering`) only when all its items are ticked, so a half-ticked merged row never shows ticked; unticking it ends the linger. Adding up is the client's: the server keeps every item as it came. Ticking a row ticks every item behind it; a row shows ticked only when all its items are. Ticked rows move to "Done".
- **By recipe**, one block per entry with its servings and a menu (open recipe, change servings, remove), then "Without a recipe" for free items; each item shows its own tick.
- Counts are **rows** everywhere: the server's `openCount`/`doneCount` on a list and on every overview card count name keys, a row open while any item behind it is unticked and done once all are (`ListShoppingLists` in SQL, `get` in Go, `TestCountsAreRowsOfMergedNames`). The list header computes the same from `rows.ts`, matching "Done · n", and the e2e spec checks that card and header agree.
- The list's menu renames it, copies it as text ("Copy as text": the open rows in aisle order, one per line, amounts first - `listText`, the shape the recipe stub copies) and deletes it.
- The side panel on a wide screen lists the recipes on the list and "Shared with" (the creator, then the members); every overview card says who shares it and when the list last changed, as a relative time (`relativeTime`).
- On a phone every target in a row is 44px without moving anything: the circle's padding is cancelled by a negative margin, the name's button stretches an `::after` over the text column and the row's padding (a tap on the amounts opens the sheet), "Clear done" grows its `::after` upward and gives its side padding back, so its text ends where the counts above end (with 200% text it takes its own line, at the end), and the header's `PeopleStack` button grows an `::after` to 44px tall; the row-wrapping geometry stays as the comment in `ShoppingRow.svelte` describes. The tick circle is centered on the name's first line through `1lh` of the name's type on its button, so it stays there at 200% text, on the list page and in the shopping mode. A row is an `@container`: below 19rem (a 360px phone, 200% text) a done row's ticker shows the face only, its name stays in the accessible label, since the name beside the source lines squeezed them to "· …". The shopping mode's title wraps to two lines (never "…"), and the close button sits on its first line; below 16rem of header (root text scaled up) the title starts beside the button instead of being centered. A recipe chip on an overview card truncates the title only, never " · servings".
- The chosen view is remembered per device (`rezepte:list-view`); the add dialog remembers the last list it added to (`rezepte:last-list`).

**The list page drops answers that arrive out of order.** Two quick taps send two requests whose answers may come back in either order. Every change takes the next revision, and only the newest change's answer replaces the list; an older answer is dropped, and so is an answer for another list, since the page stays mounted when one list links to the next. A failed change undoes its own optimistic step only while nothing newer was sent, and reloads otherwise. Ticks and department moves are optimistic.

**The add dialog starts from the recipe page.** Only the signed-in recipe page offers the basket (`addable` on `RecipeView` and `IngredientList`); the public share page keeps the copy button but has no basket, since its recipe id is client-only and an anonymous reader has no lists. `AddToListDialog` opens at the reader's current servings with every ingredient picked except the ones ticked off on the recipe page; it can create a list inline, and a failed add after a created list retries on that list rather than making a second one. The toast after adding links to the list.

**Navigation and people.** Shopping lists are a top-bar link on a computer and a bottom-nav slot on a phone ([Design system](/architecture/design-system)). The people page shows each person's number of lists they started, from `CountsByCreator`, never their content ([Users and authentication](/features/users-and-auth)).

## Why it is this way

**Copy on add, because a recipe save rewrites its ingredient rows.** `recipe.Service.Update` deletes and re-inserts ingredients on every save, so an item pointing at an ingredient row would break on the next edit; and a list someone is shopping from must not change under them because an author fixed a typo. To pick up a correction one adds the recipe again.

**Scaling lives in Go too, because more than the browser adds at servings.** Changing servings on the list, and adding through the API, both need scaled amounts the server computes; the stored quantity stays unscaled so that a servings change is exact rather than a rescale of a rounded number.

**404 rather than 403**, because a 403 confirms the id exists and belongs to someone.

**An invitation, not an add**, because a list holds what a person shops for and a stranger's list in your overview is noise until you want it; accepting is the consent, declining costs the inviter nothing and can be retried.

**Admins stay out.** Access is the predicate above and nothing else: an admin sees a list only as its creator or member, so the privacy sentence in the user guide holds without exceptions.

**Overrides are instance-wide**, because one person teaching Rezepte where capers go helps everyone on the instance; a per-person or per-list override would have to be taught again by each.

**The word list is a short table, not a dictionary library.** A stemmer or lemma library is a dependency against "standard library first", and the ingredients that matter are a few hundred words.

## Rejected

- **Items referencing ingredient rows** - see the copy rule above.
- **A dictionary or stemming library for name keys** - see the word list above.

## Limits

- Deleting an account publishes nothing on the stream: a list it owned stays on a member's open page until the next change or reconnect brings the 404, and a list it was a member of shows it in "Shared with" until the next refetch.
- Irregular plurals beyond `irregularPlurals` stay separate keys, and a changed `NameKey` does not rewrite stored keys.
- An override is per name key, not per list or person, and nothing lists or resets overrides except moving the item again.
- Free-text parsing is naive: "Salz, Pfeffer" becomes name "Salz" plus note "Pfeffer", "2 - 3 Zitronen" splits at the dash, and a bare "pepper" is pantry rather than spice.
