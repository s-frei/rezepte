---
title: Cook timers
description: One tap on a step's stored time starts a timer; the cook-mode dock, the alarm banner, the player outside cook mode, held in the browser, and a Web Push when it ends
---

A cook who reads "90 Minuten schmoren" in cook mode taps the time and a timer runs. Several can run at once, from one step, several steps or several recipes; they stay visible while the cook pages through the steps, leaves cook mode or reloads, and an ended timer rings until it is turned off. A timer lives only in the browser that started it; the server only sends the push that tells a locked phone it ended. It runs on the seconds a [step time](/features/step-times) stores, never on text parsed in cook mode.

## How it works

**The store is one app-wide `$state` class.** `Timers` in `frontend/src/lib/recipe/timers.svelte.ts` holds the list and a `now` that one interval ticks once a second, alive only while a timer runs. It writes the list to `localStorage` (`TIMERS_STORAGE_KEY`) on every change, each access wrapped in `try`/`catch` like the servings key, so blocked storage leaves the timers running in memory for the page view. Remaining time is always `endsAt` against `Date.now()`, so a reload, a background tab or a suspended phone resumes exactly; `settle` turns a running timer whose `endsAt` passed into a ringing one, on load and on every tick. The pure rules (`timerId`, `remainingMs`, `fraction`, `byEnding`, `parseTimers`, `formatClock`, `clampStep`) live in `timers.ts` with unit tests; the store's behavior is tested in `timers-store.test.ts`.

**A timer is recipe + step + phrase.** `timerId` joins the three, and `start` returns the existing timer for an id it already holds, so a second tap - also on a paused or ringing timer - starts nothing. A step with two times gets two timers. The timer stores the recipe's slug and title and the step's number and phrase, and is named from those (`timer_name`, "Schritt 5 · 90 Minuten"); a recipe edited while it runs changes nothing about it.

**In cook mode each stored time is a button.** `StepText` takes `onstarttime` and `activeTimer` only from the cook route: with them a time renders as `<button data-time>` labeled `timer_start`, with the same look as the plain mark and an `::after` that makes its target taller without moving a letter (never wider: a sideways overhang widens the step past its column on a narrow phone), and a time whose timer exists swaps its clock glyph for a `TimerRing`. The detail page passes neither and renders the time as before. `segmentStep` hands each time segment its `StepTime`, so the button starts the stored `seconds` - a range runs on the lower bound, never `maxSeconds`. The tap calls `unlockAudio()` first: it is the gesture browsers want before they play the alarm.

**The dock sits between the step and the footer.** `TimerDock` lists every non-ringing timer by `byEnding` - least time left first, paused or not, so a ringing one (none left) leads wherever it is listed - of every recipe; a row of another recipe adds that recipe's title on a third line of its own, so it never cuts the name (`data-timer-name`), which is what tells two timers of one recipe apart. Below `md` it starts collapsed into one capsule as wide as the footer (`TimerSummary`: the first timer's ring with `+N` for the others sitting on it as a badge, its time, its name and below that "fertig 19:42" or "pausiert" - `timerStatus` in `status.ts` - plus another recipe's title) and expands into a panel of rows on `user-teal`, the capsule's own color; the state is a plain `$state`, held for the visit, and focus moves to the counterpart button when the pressed one disappears. From `md` the rows are always shown, two side by side, and a single one is as wide as the footer under it (`max-w-[392px]`), so it lines up with Back and Next. A row's ring is the pause button (⏸ while running, ▶ while paused, labeled `timer_pause` / `timer_resume`); its text opens the timer's step - `go` to the step clamped by `clampStep` for this recipe, `goto(cookPath(t))` otherwise; ✕ stops it. While focus is inside the dock the page's arrow and Escape handler stands down; elsewhere the arrows keep paging.

**An ended timer becomes a banner under the progress bar.** `TimerBanner` (on `primary`, bell, `timer_done`, the name, `timer_off`) renders once per ringing timer, stacked, on every width, and leaves the dock; another recipe's title stands on a line of its own under the name. It carries `role="alert"`, so a screen reader hears the end once. Start, pause and resume reach it through the dock's polite live region, fed by `timers.listen`; the countdown itself is never announced. The chime and vibration belong to the root layout, which rings while `timers.ringing` is not empty and stops after `CHIME_LIMIT_MS`; the banner stays until "Aus".

**Outside cook mode the shell shows every timer behind one capsule.** `TimerIndicator`, rendered by `AppShell` (so never in cook mode, on the login pages or on a public share), shows the first of `byEnding` in a `user-teal` capsule (`TimerSummary` with the recipe title above the name): below `md` a player across the bottom, above the bottom nav where `AppShell` renders one (`aboveNav`) and above the screen's edge otherwise, always as wide as the bar: it reads the nav's `bottomNav.docked` and `bottomNav.still` and takes the bar's floating width (95% of `inset-x-2`, lifted) or its docked one, 8px above it, with the bar's spring, so the two move as one stack; from `md` fixed at the bottom right. A tap on it toggles a panel above it, `user-teal` like the capsule, listing every timer - ringing ones as `TimerBanner`s, the rest as `TimerRow`s with the recipe title, linked (`link`) to `cookPath(t)`; the chevron points up and turns when open. Escape (focus back on the capsule), a pointer down outside it and every navigation (`afterNavigate`, which a row's link is) close the panel. A ringing first timer turns the capsule `primary` with two lines, "Fertig · " and the recipe title over the name, and an "Aus" button; the title gives way, the name stays whole at 320 px. Like the nav, the player steps aside on a phone while a field that brings up the software keyboard has focus (`isField` in `frontend/src/lib/components/shell/typing.ts`, shared with `BottomNav`). While a timer runs, `AppShell` ends the page above the player with a larger bottom padding (`pb-36` with the nav, `pb-24` without, `md:pb-24`), and the root layout gives the Toaster larger `offset` and `mobileOffset` values, so toasts stack above it. The open panel lies over the page, it does not push it. Nothing at the top of a page knows the player exists: only the nav shares the bottom with it, and both belong to the shell.

**`?step=N` opens cook mode at that step.** `cookPath(t)` in `frontend/src/lib/recipe/timers.ts` builds `/recipes/<slug>/cook?step=<n>` for the rows, the dock and the push alarm alike. The cook route keeps the parameter as the step on screen (see [Cooking](/features/cooking)): it reads it through `clampStep`, so a step removed since the timer started lands on the last step, and rewrites it on every step change. Jumping from one recipe's cook mode to another's reuses the page component, so `afterNavigate` reads the parameter again for a navigation that stays on the cook route.

**An ended timer reaches a hidden page through Web Push, in three tiers.** With the page in view the banner and the chime are all there is. With the page in the background or the phone locked, the server pushes a notification at `endsAt`: one path for both, so a timer never notifies twice. Without push (plain HTTP, a browser without `PushManager`, iOS outside the home-screen app, or notifications denied) only the page tells, and the dock says so once under its rows (`timer_push_unavailable`, `data-push-unavailable`).

**The page arms and disarms the alarms.** `frontend/src/lib/push.svelte.ts`, imported once by the root layout, registers `/service-worker.js` when `pushSupported()` (secure context, `serviceWorker`, `PushManager`, `Notification`) - `type: 'module'` in dev, `'classic'` in the build, since `kit.serviceWorker.register` is off in `vite.config.ts`. It listens on `timers.listen`. On `start` and `resume` it asks `Notification.requestPermission()` only while the permission is `default`, synchronously inside the tap's call stack (`start` runs from the click; a prompt outside a gesture is refused), so nothing asks on page load. Granted, it subscribes once per page view (`GET /api/v1/push/key`, `pushManager.subscribe`, `PUT /api/v1/push/subscription`) and posts `POST /api/v1/push/alarms` with `fireAt = endsAt`, title `timer_notify_title` (the recipe), body `timer_name` and url `/recipes/<slug>/cook?step=<n>`, all in the member's UI language, so the server needs no catalog; the returned id goes on the timer as `alarmId` via `timers.setAlarm`. An answer that arrives after the timer was paused or stopped is cancelled at once. On `pause` and `stop` it sends `DELETE /api/v1/push/alarms/{id}` and closes a shown notification with that tag (`registration.getNotifications`). Every failure is swallowed - offline, a 422 for a push host outside the server's allowlist, a browser without a push service - and the in-page timer runs on unchanged. The client is `frontend/src/lib/api/push.ts`.

**The service worker only shows and opens.** `frontend/src/service-worker.ts` (SvelteKit's, built to `/service-worker.js`, which `spa.go` serves `no-cache` so a new worker reaches devices at once) has `push` and `notificationclick` and nothing else: no `fetch` handler, no cache. `push` parses `{title, body, url, tag}` with `parsePayload` (in `frontend/src/lib/push-worker.ts`, unit-tested; a url must stay on the origin), and `notificationOptions` decides: with any window client visible the notification is `silent` and closed at once, because the page already rings and Chromium wants a notification shown for every push. A click focuses an open window and `navigate`s it to the url, or opens a new window; `clients.claim()` on activate makes the open pages ones `navigate` may move. The cook route's `?step=` then lands on the step.

**The server keeps devices and alarms, never timers.** `service/internal/push` stores `push_subscriptions` (one endpoint per account at a time; moving an endpoint to another account drops the previous account's alarms) and `timer_alarms`, with at most 20 subscriptions per user and 50 alarms per subscription. An endpoint must be `https` on a known push host (FCM, Mozilla, Apple, Windows), or the member could make the server POST to an internal host (`TestSubscribeRejectsForeignEndpoint`). `fireAt` lies at most a minute past and seven days ahead; the url starts with `/recipes/`. A cancel of an alarm already sent, another account's or never there is success (`TestCancelMissingIsNoError`). `Service.Start` ticks every 5 seconds: each due alarm is deleted before it is sent, so there is no retry and no double send, one more than an hour late is dropped unsent, and a push service answering `404` or `410` takes the subscription with it. The payload is encrypted (RFC 8291, `aes128gcm`) and signed (VAPID, RFC 8292) with the standard library in `webpush.go`, tested against the RFC vectors. The VAPID key is generated and stored in `instance_settings.vapid_key` the first time it is needed, not at startup; its subject is `REZEPTE_PUBLIC_URL` when that is `https`, a `mailto:` otherwise. The routes are session-only, tag `push`.

**Sign-out cancels the pushes, then clears every timer.** `signOut` in `frontend/src/lib/sign-out.ts` awaits `disablePush()` - cancel each timer's alarm, `DELETE /api/v1/push/subscription`, `subscription.unsubscribe()`, each failure swallowed - and then calls `timers.clear()`, both before the logout request, while the session still exists. `disablePush` drops the `alarmId`s first, so the `stop` events of `clear()` send nothing after the session is gone. The storage key carries no user id: a shared kitchen tablet starts clean for the next person, and nobody's timer rings for someone who already left.

**The e2e suite drives a paused page clock.** `frontend/e2e/cook-timers.test.ts` installs `page.clock` before the first navigation, pauses it once signed in and moves it with `runFor`, under `reducedMotion: 'reduce'` so Svelte's transitions do not wait on the paused clock. Two traps: `expandDock` first waits for the dock, because `isVisible()` does not wait and on a phone the capsule would be skipped before the recipe renders; and a client-side link navigation stalls while the clock is paused, so the tests that follow a timer's link resume it before the click. It reads German through a German account and the German fixtures, which carry stored times ("15 Minuten" in Königsberger Klopse step 3, "90 Minuten" in Rinderrouladen step 5). The context grants `notifications`, yet headless Chromium still reports `Notification.permission` as `denied` (only `permissions.query` says `granted`), so the dock shows the push line there; the test that checks the alarm request overrides `Notification.permission` and fakes `PushManager.subscribe`, since Playwright's Chromium has no push service, and stubs the two push routes. The user-docs screenshot `cook-mode-timers` overrides the permission the same way, so the picture shows a device that allowed notifications. The push store itself is tested in `push-store.test.ts` with stubbed `navigator.serviceWorker`, `Notification` and `fetch`.

## Why it is this way

- **State in the browser, time from `endsAt`.** A timer is a promise about this kitchen, not household data; nothing on the server needs it, and deriving the remaining time from a timestamp is the only form that survives a sleeping phone.
- **The dock collapses on phones.** At 320 px and the default type size a panel of rows would take the step's room; one capsule keeps the step readable and still says how much is left on the timer that matters next.
- **The ring is the pause button, with a glyph.** It is where the eye already is; without ⏸/▶ in its center nobody would guess a ring is tappable.
- **The banner at the top, under the progress bar.** It is where the cook looks first, it leaves the footer's "Weiter" where the thumb expects it, and the running timers stay in the dock below.
- **One push path for a hidden page and a locked phone.** A `Notification` from page JS for the background tab would be a second notification beside the push; one path means exactly one per timer.
- **The copy is built in the browser.** The member's language is known there; the server sends what it was given and needs no message catalog.
- **A visible window gets a silent notification, closed at once.** Skipping it is not an option: Chromium shows its own "updated in the background" notice for a push that showed nothing.
- **Least time left, not soonest end.** The capsule answers "what needs me next"; a paused timer with four minutes left is closer to that than a running one with ten.
- **The capsule opens a list outside cook mode, it does not jump.** Several timers of several recipes run at once; a capsule that only led to the first left the others out of reach, and pause and stop belong where the timers are seen.
- **One teal capsule everywhere.** The phone player, the desktop capsule and the dock are one thing in three places, panels included; a panel on `accent` also disappeared into the `accent` placeholders of recipes without a photo.
- **The player sits at the bottom, above the nav.** It is the mini player of a music app: the bottom belongs to the shell alone, while the top of a page is where pages put their own fixed and sticky heads (the detail page's bar, the settings and editor section pickers); anything at the top has to be known to every one of them, and one that forgets is covered.

## Rejected

- **A text countdown in the step instead of a dock.** The step text would reflow every second and a timer would vanish with its step.
- **A kitchen dial.** Charming, but a dial says "about ten minutes", and the cook wants the exact remaining time at a glance.
- **The alarm as a card at the bottom or in place of the footer.** Below the step it is out of sight, and replacing "Weiter" puts an off switch where the thumb pages.
- **An island at the top center of a phone (outside cook mode).** Every fixed or sticky head had to move down by its height through a shared variable; the ones that did not were covered, and content scrolled through the gap above the detail page's bar. A solid band at the top behind it would close the gap but keeps the same rule for every head, and costs its height on every page.
- **A notch hanging from the top edge (outside cook mode).** It reads as system chrome and sits where the phone's own status bar crowds it.
- **The indicator in the header's center on desktop.** The top bar's middle holds the breadcrumb and page title; a timer there crowds both, and the bottom-right corner is free.
- **Starting timers outside cook mode, or live rings on the detail page.** The detail page is for reading and planning; one indicator outside cook mode is enough to see what runs.
- **Asking for notifications on page load or in a settings switch.** A prompt nobody asked for gets denied for good; the first timer start is the moment the cook wants one.
- **Caching in the service worker.** The worker exists for push; a cache would bring invalidation for an app that works only online anyway.
- **Timer state on the server.** Only the alarm is needed for the push; the timers stay a promise about this kitchen.
- **A per-user storage key.** A timer still ringing for someone who signed out is worse than the next person starting clean.

## Limits

- Timers live per device and per browser; another device of the same household never sees them.
- A range ("20 bis 25 Minuten") runs on its lower bound, with no choice offered.
- A timer that ended while the page was closed comes back ringing, but the browser plays no sound before the first tap after a reload; the banner or the player shows the end silently before that tap.
- The chime gives up after `CHIME_LIMIT_MS`; only the banner or the player keeps showing the end.
- Push needs a secure context: on plain HTTP there is no service worker and no notification while the page is hidden. On iPhone and iPad push works only in the installed home-screen app (iOS 16.4 and later).
- A denied permission is not asked again; the dock's line stays until the cook allows notifications in the browser's site settings.
- The alarm is fixed when the timer starts or resumes: a phone whose clock is off by a minute gets the push a minute off from the in-page banner.
- An alarm is sent once, never retried; one more than an hour late (the server was down) is dropped.
- Verified: the alarm request and the worker's registration in Chromium under Playwright, and the worker's payload rules by unit test. Unverified on a device: delivery to a locked iPhone home-screen app and a locked Android phone, and the system notification on desktop Chrome with the tab hidden.
