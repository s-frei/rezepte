---
title: Cook timers
description: One tap on a step's stored time starts a timer; the cook-mode dock, the alarm banner, the player outside cook mode, held in the browser, and a Web Push when it ends
---

A cook who reads "90 Minuten schmoren" in cook mode taps the time and a timer runs. Several can run at once, from one step, several steps or several recipes; they stay visible while the cook pages through the steps, leaves cook mode or reloads, and an ended timer rings until it is turned off. A timer lives only in the browser that started it; the server only sends the push that tells a locked phone it ended. It runs on the seconds a [step time](/features/step-times) stores, never on text parsed in cook mode. The store and its rules are in `frontend/src/lib/recipe/`, the dock, banner and player in `frontend/src/lib/components/timer/`, the browser's push side in `frontend/src/lib/push.svelte.ts` and the server's in `service/internal/push`.

<Callout type="warn" title="Traps">

- `Notification.requestPermission()` must run synchronously inside the tap's call stack; a prompt outside a gesture is refused ([Push from the page](#push-from-the-page)).
- Never subscribe or call a push route without a session or on a `/s/` share: the routes answer `401` and `api()` sends a visitor to the login page ([Push from the page](#push-from-the-page)).
- A push on a visible page must still show a notification, then close it: Chromium shows its own "updated in the background" notice for a push that showed nothing ([The service worker](#the-service-worker)).
- `signOut` must cancel the alarms before `timers.clear()` and before the logout request, while the session exists ([Sign-out](#sign-out)).

</Callout>

## How it works

### The store

**The store is one app-wide `$state` class.** `Timers` in `frontend/src/lib/recipe/timers.svelte.ts` holds the list and a `now` that one interval ticks once a second, alive only while a timer runs. It writes the list to `localStorage` (`TIMERS_STORAGE_KEY`) on every change through `load` and `save` from `#lib/storage` like the servings key, so blocked storage leaves the timers running in memory for the page view. Remaining time is always `endsAt` against `Date.now()`, so a reload, a background tab or a suspended phone resumes exactly; `settle` turns a running timer whose `endsAt` passed into a ringing one, on load and on every tick. The pure rules (`timerId`, `remainingMs`, `fraction`, `byEnding`, `parseTimers`, `formatClock`, `clampStep`) live in `timers.ts` with unit tests; the store's behavior is tested in `timers-store.test.ts`.

**A timer is recipe + step + phrase.** `timerId` joins the three, and `start` returns the existing timer for an id it already holds, so a second tap - also on a paused or ringing timer - starts nothing. A step with two times gets two timers. The timer stores the recipe's slug and title and the step's number and phrase, and is named from those (`timer_name`, "Schritt 5 · 90 Minuten"); a recipe edited while it runs changes nothing about it.

### Starting one in cook mode

**In cook mode each stored time is a button; on the detail page it is not.** `StepText` takes `onstarttime` and `activeTimer` only from the cook route: with them a time renders as `<button data-time>` labeled `timer_start`, a `user-teal` chip whose play disc goes once its timer exists and whose clock glyph then turns into a `TimerRing`. The detail page passes neither and keeps the dotted underline, the mark of a time that is not tappable. The chip's enlarged tap target grows only vertically ([Targets and controls](/architecture/design-system#targets-and-controls)). `segmentStep` hands each time segment its `StepTime`, so the button starts the stored `seconds` - a range runs on the lower bound, never `maxSeconds`. The tap calls `unlockAudio()` first: it is the gesture browsers want before they play the alarm.

### The dock

**The dock (`TimerDock`) sits between the step and the footer and lists every non-ringing timer of every recipe by `byEnding`** - least time left first, paused or not. A row of another recipe adds that recipe's title on a line of its own (`data-timer-name`), so it never cuts the name, which is what tells two timers of one recipe apart. Below `md` the dock starts collapsed into one capsule as wide as the footer (`TimerSummary`: the first timer's ring with a `+N` badge, its time, its name, and "fertig 19:42" or "pausiert" from `timerStatus` in `status.ts`) and expands into a panel of rows; the state is a plain `$state`, held for the visit, and focus moves to the counterpart button when the pressed one disappears. From `md` the rows are always shown, two side by side, and a single one is as wide as the footer under it, so it lines up with Back and Next.

**A row's ring is the pause button, its text opens the step, ✕ stops it.** The ring shows ⏸ or ▶ (`timer_pause` / `timer_resume`); the text `go`es to the step clamped by `clampStep` for this recipe, or `goto(cookPath(t))` for another. While focus is inside the dock the page's arrow and Escape handler stands down; elsewhere the arrows keep paging.

### The alarm banner

**An ended timer leaves the dock and becomes a `TimerBanner` under the progress bar**, one per ringing timer, stacked, on every width, with `timer_done`, the name, another recipe's title and `timer_off`. It carries `role="alert"`, so a screen reader hears the end once. Start, pause and resume reach screen readers through the dock's polite live region, fed by `timers.listen`; the countdown itself is never announced. The chime and vibration belong to the root layout, which rings while `timers.ringing` is not empty and stops after `CHIME_LIMIT_MS`; the banner stays until "Aus". While it rings, the bell swings and a halo breathes (`bell-swing`, `ringing-halo` in `app.css`, kept under reduced motion), but the banner itself stays still so "Aus" never moves under the finger.

### The player outside cook mode

**Outside cook mode `TimerIndicator` shows every timer behind one capsule.** `AppShell` renders it, so it never appears in cook mode, on the login pages or on a public share. It shows the first of `byEnding` in a `user-teal` `TimerSummary` with the recipe title: below `md` as a player across the bottom, above the bottom nav where `AppShell` renders one (`aboveNav`) and above the screen's edge otherwise; from `md` fixed at the bottom right. On a phone it reads the nav's `bottomNav.docked` and `bottomNav.still` and takes the bar's width and spring, so the two move as one stack, and it steps aside with the nav while a field that brings up the software keyboard has focus (`bottomNav.typing`, tracked by `AppShell` with `isField` from `frontend/src/lib/components/shell/typing.ts`).

**A tap toggles a panel listing every timer**: ringing ones as `TimerBanner`s, the rest as `TimerRow`s linked to `cookPath(t)`. Escape (focus back on the capsule), a pointer down outside and every navigation (`afterNavigate`) close it. The panel only toggles `hidden`, so `aria-controls` always finds it, and it lies over the page instead of pushing it. A ringing first timer turns the capsule `primary` with "Fertig · ", the recipe title, the name and an "Aus" button; at 320 px the title gives way and the name stays whole. When the capsule turns to ringing, its text color switches at the midpoint of the background crossfade rather than fading with it, because a faded ink all but vanishes in the middle.

**Only the shell knows the player is there.** While a timer runs, `AppShell` gives the page a larger bottom padding and the root layout gives the Toaster larger `offset` and `mobileOffset` values, so content and toasts end above it. Nothing at the top of a page knows about it: only the nav shares the bottom, and both belong to the shell. Another tab can start a timer (via `storage`), so the player rises in then; a page that loads with timers running shows it at once.

### Deep links into a step

**`?step=N` opens cook mode at that step.** `cookPath(t)` in `frontend/src/lib/recipe/timers.ts` builds `/recipes/<slug>/cook?step=<n>` for the rows, the dock and the push alarm alike. The cook route keeps the parameter as the step on screen (see [Cooking](/features/cooking)): it reads it through `clampStep`, so a step removed since the timer started lands on the last step, and rewrites it on every step change. Jumping from one recipe's cook mode to another's reuses the page component, so `afterNavigate` reads the parameter again for a navigation that stays on the cook route.

### Push from the page

**An ended timer reaches a hidden page through Web Push, in three tiers.** With the page in view the banner and the chime are all there is. With the page in the background or the phone locked, the server pushes a notification at `endsAt`: one path for both, so a timer never notifies twice. Without push (plain HTTP, a browser without `PushManager`, iOS outside the home-screen app, or notifications denied) only the page tells, and the dock says so once under its rows (`timer_push_unavailable`, `data-push-unavailable`).

**`frontend/src/lib/push.svelte.ts`, imported once by the root layout, registers the worker and asks for permission only from a tap.** It registers `/service-worker.js` when `pushSupported()` (secure context, `serviceWorker`, `PushManager`, `Notification`) as a module (`type: 'module'`, the way SvelteKit builds it), by hand since `serviceWorker.register` is off in `vite.config.ts`. It listens on `timers.listen`. On `start` and `resume` it asks `Notification.requestPermission()` only while the permission is `default`, synchronously inside the tap's call stack (`start` runs from the click; a prompt outside a gesture is refused), so nothing asks on page load.

**It subscribes once per sign-in, never without a session.** It subscribes (`GET /api/v1/push/key`, `pushManager.subscribe`, `PUT /api/v1/push/subscription`) when someone signs in while the permission is already `granted`, otherwise after the tap's grant. Signing in is `onSignIn` in `frontend/src/lib/auth.svelte.ts`: `session` is a getter and setter over one `$state`, and assigning a user with another id than before calls the listeners, so the layout's `me()`, the login form and a sign-in as someone else each subscribe once, and saving the same account's profile does not. Never without a session and never on a `/s/` share: signed out, the push routes answer `401`, and `api()` would send a visitor on `/welcome`, `/unsubscribe` or a share to the login page (`push-store.test.ts`). The `PUT` is idempotent and moves the endpoint to whoever is signed in now; a failure is swallowed and the next tap tries again.

**Each timer schedules one alarm and cancels it on pause and stop.** Every timer shares the one subscription and posts `POST /api/v1/push/alarms` with `fireAt = endsAt`, title `timer_notify_title` (the recipe), body `timer_name` and url `/recipes/<slug>/cook?step=<n>`, all in the member's UI language, so the server needs no catalog; the returned id goes on the timer as `alarmId` via `timers.setAlarm`. An answer that arrives after the timer was paused or stopped is canceled at once. On `pause` and `stop` it sends `DELETE /api/v1/push/alarms/{id}` and closes a shown notification with that tag (`registration.getNotifications`). Every failure is swallowed - offline, a 422 for a push host outside the server's allowlist, a browser without a push service - and the in-page timer runs on unchanged. The client is `frontend/src/lib/api/push.ts`.

### The service worker

**The service worker only shows and opens.** `frontend/src/service-worker/index.ts` (SvelteKit's, built to `/service-worker.js`, which `spa.go` serves `no-cache` so a new worker reaches devices at once) has `push` and `notificationclick` and nothing else: no `fetch` handler, no cache. `push` parses the alarm's `{kind: 'timer', title, body, url, tag}` with `parsePayload` (in `frontend/src/lib/push-worker.ts`, unit-tested; a url must stay on the origin; the notification pushes it shows too are in [Installable app](/features/installable-app)), and `notificationOptions` decides: with any window client visible a timer's notification is `silent` and closed at once, because the page already rings and Chromium wants a notification shown for every push. A click focuses an open window and `navigate`s it to the url, or opens a new window; `clients.claim()` on activate makes the open pages ones `navigate` may move. The cook route's `?step=` then lands on the step.

### The server

**The server keeps devices and alarms, never timers.** `service/internal/push` stores `push_subscriptions` and `timer_alarms` ([Data model](/architecture/data-model)). Each endpoint belongs to one account at a time; a `PUT` of an endpoint already stored moves that row to the caller's account and session in place, so its alarms stay: a timer started before a switch on the device still rings, and the person now signed in can cancel it (`TestSubscribeMovesEndpointWithItsAlarms`). A subscription belongs to the session whose `PUT` stored it (`auth.SessionIDFrom`) and is deleted with it, its alarms too: a device signed out, or ended by a password change, a reset or an admin, gets no more pushes, and `SendToUser` skips a session already past its expiry (`TestSubscriptionEndsWithSession`, `TestSendToUserSkipsEndedSessions`). Signing in again stores it again with the new session.

**The server validates every subscription and alarm.**

- Each member has at most 20 subscriptions and 50 alarms per subscription; only a new endpoint counts, so a device moving over from another account never hits the quota.
- An endpoint must be `https` on a known push host (FCM at `fcm.googleapis.com` and `jmt17.google.com`, where newer Chrome versions subscribe, Mozilla, Apple, Windows; one exact Google host, never all of `google.com`), or the member could make the server POST to an internal host (`TestSubscribeRejectsForeignEndpoint`).
- `fireAt` lies at most a minute past and seven days ahead; the url starts with `/recipes/`.
- A cancel of an alarm already sent, another account's or never there is success (`TestCancelMissingIsNoError`).

**`Service.Start` ticks every 5 seconds and sends each due alarm at most once.** Each due alarm is deleted before it is sent, so there is no retry and no double send; one more than an hour late is dropped unsent, and a push service answering `404` or `410` takes the subscription with it. Every push carries one JSON payload, `push.Message`: `kind`, `title`, `body`, `url`, `tag` and an optional `collapse` (`title` and `bodyMany` with a literal `{count}` the service worker fills); an alarm sends `kind: "timer"` with its id as the tag.

**`SendToUser` fans one notification out to every device of a member.** Each send has its own 10-second timeout, and a `404`/`410` drops that one device while the rest still get it (`TestSendToUserDropsGoneKeepsOthers`); a device another member signed in on no longer gets the first account's pushes, because `PUT /push/subscription` moved the endpoint (`TestSendToUserAfterAccountMove`). What it sends is in [Notifications](/features/notifications).

**`POST /api/v1/push/test` pushes to the caller's own device only.** It takes the caller's endpoint and sends `kind: "test"`, tag `test`, copy `mail_push_test_title`/`_body` from the mail catalog in the member's language (`mail.Text`) and url `/settings/notifications`. Another account's or an unknown endpoint is `404`, and so is one the push service answers `404` or `410` for, whose row goes as in the fan-out (`TestSendTestGoneIsUnknown`), so the settings show the device as off; a second test for one endpoint within 10 seconds is `429` (`ErrTooSoon`, an in-memory map a restart forgets).

**Encryption and signing use the standard library.** The payload is encrypted (RFC 8291, `aes128gcm`) and signed (VAPID, RFC 8292) in `webpush.go`, tested against the RFC vectors. The VAPID key is generated and stored in `instance_settings.vapid_key` the first time it is needed, not at startup; its subject is `REZEPTE_PUBLIC_URL` when that is `https`, a `mailto:` otherwise. The routes are session-only, tag `push`.

### Sign-out

**Sign-out cancels the pushes, then clears every timer.** `signOut` in `frontend/src/lib/sign-out.ts` awaits `disablePush()` - cancel each timer's alarm, `DELETE /api/v1/push/subscription`, `subscription.unsubscribe()`, each failure swallowed - and then calls `timers.clear()`, both before the logout request, while the session still exists. `disablePush` drops the `alarmId`s first, so the `stop` events of `clear()` send nothing after the session is gone. The storage key carries no user id: a shared kitchen tablet starts clean for the next person, and nobody's timer rings for someone who already left.

### Tests

**The e2e suite drives a paused page clock** by the rules of [A paused page clock](/conventions/testing#a-paused-page-clock). `frontend/e2e/cook-timers.test.ts` pauses it once signed in; a jump with `fastForward` shows the same as ticking, since the dock reads the time left from the clock. `expandDock` waits for the dock before it asks whether the phone's capsule is there, and the tests that follow a timer's link resume the clock before the click. The suite reads German through a German account and the German fixtures, which carry stored times ("15 Minuten" in Königsberger Klopse step 3, "90 Minuten" in Rinderrouladen step 5).

**Push is faked in the browser tests.** Headless Chromium reports no notification permission and has no push service ([Verify UI](/howtos/verify-ui#what-playwright-cannot-do)), so the dock shows the push line there; the test that checks the alarm request overrides `Notification.permission`, fakes `PushManager.subscribe` and stubs the two push routes. The user-docs screenshot `cook-mode-timers` overrides the permission the same way, so the picture shows a device that allowed notifications. The push store itself is tested in `push-store.test.ts` with stubbed `navigator.serviceWorker`, `Notification` and `fetch`.

## Why it is this way

- **State in the browser, time from `endsAt`.** A timer is a promise about this kitchen, not household data; nothing on the server needs it, and deriving the remaining time from a timestamp is the only form that survives a sleeping phone.
- **The dock collapses on phones.** At 320 px and the default type size a panel of rows would take the step's room; one capsule keeps the step readable and still says how much is left on the timer that matters next.
- **The ring is the pause button, with a glyph.** It is where the eye already is; without ⏸/▶ in its center nobody would guess a ring is tappable.
- **The banner at the top, under the progress bar.** It is where the cook looks first, it leaves the footer's "Weiter" where the thumb expects it, and the running timers stay in the dock below.
- **One push path for a hidden page and a locked phone.** A `Notification` from page JS for the background tab would be a second notification beside the push; one path means exactly one per timer.
- **A timer's copy is built in the browser.** The member's language and the timer are known there; the server sends an alarm as it was given. A notification's copy is built on the server instead, from the mail catalog, because no page is involved when it is sent ([Notifications](/features/notifications)).
- **A visible window gets a silent notification, closed at once.** Skipping it is not an option: Chromium shows its own "updated in the background" notice for a push that showed nothing.
- **Least time left, not soonest end.** The capsule answers "what needs me next"; a paused timer with four minutes left is closer to that than a running one with ten.
- **The capsule opens a list outside cook mode, it does not jump.** Several timers of several recipes run at once; a capsule that only led to the first left the others out of reach, and pause and stop belong where the timers are seen.
- **One teal capsule everywhere.** The phone player, the desktop capsule and the dock are one thing in three places, panels included; a panel on `accent` also disappeared into the `accent` placeholders of recipes without a photo.
- **The player sits at the bottom, above the nav.** It is the mini player of a music app: the bottom belongs to the shell alone, while the top of a page is where pages put their own fixed and sticky heads (the detail page's bar, the settings and editor section pickers); anything at the top has to be known to every one of them, and one that forgets is covered.

## Rejected

- **A text countdown in the step instead of a dock.** The step text would reflow every second and a timer would vanish with its step.
- **A kitchen dial.** Charming, but a dial says "about ten minutes", and the cook wants the exact remaining time at a glance.
- **The alarm as a card at the bottom or in place of the footer.** Below the step it is out of sight, and replacing "Weiter" puts an off switch where the thumb pages.
- **An island at the top center of a phone (outside cook mode).** Every fixed or sticky head had to move down by its height through a shared variable; the ones that did not were covered, and content scrolled through the gap above the detail page's bar. A solid band at the top behind it would close the gap but keeps the same rule for every head, and costs its height on every page.
- **A notch hanging from the top edge (outside cook mode).** It reads as system chrome and sits where the phone's own status bar crowds it.
- **The indicator in the header's center on desktop.** The top bar's middle holds the places, and the breadcrumb and page title sit beside the logo; a timer there crowds both, and the bottom-right corner is free.
- **Starting timers outside cook mode, or live rings on the detail page.** The detail page is for reading and planning; one indicator outside cook mode is enough to see what runs.
- **Asking for notifications on page load.** A prompt nobody asked for gets denied for good; it comes only from a tap that wants notifications: the first timer start, or "Enable on this device" in Settings → Notifications.
- **Caching in the service worker.** The worker exists for push; a cache would bring invalidation for an app that works only online anyway.
- **Timer state on the server.** Only the alarm is needed for the push; the timers stay a promise about this kitchen.
- **A per-user storage key.** A timer still ringing for someone who signed out is worse than the next person starting clean.

## Limits

- Timers live per device and per browser; another device of the same household never sees them.
- A range ("20 bis 25 Minuten") runs on its lower bound, with no choice offered.
- A timer that ended while the page was closed comes back ringing, but the browser plays no sound before the first tap after a reload; the banner or the player shows the end silently before that tap.
- The chime gives up after `CHIME_LIMIT_MS`; only the banner or the player keeps showing the end.
- Push needs a secure context: on plain HTTP there is no service worker and no notification while the page is hidden. On iPhone and iPad push works only in the installed home-screen app (iOS 16.4 and later).
- A denied permission is not asked again; the dock's line stays until the cook allows notifications in the browser's site settings.
- The alarm is fixed when the timer starts or resumes: a phone whose clock is off by a minute gets the push a minute off from the in-page banner.
- An alarm is sent once, never retried; one more than an hour late (the server was down) is dropped.
- Verified: the alarm request and the worker's registration in Chromium under Playwright, and the worker's payload rules by unit test. Unverified on a device: delivery to a locked iPhone home-screen app and a locked Android phone, and the system notification on desktop Chrome with the tab hidden.
