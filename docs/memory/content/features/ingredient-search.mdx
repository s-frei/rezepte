A reader picks the ingredients they have as chips in the overview's search field and sees which recipes use most of them, what each still needs, and ticks off what is at home until a card reads "All there". Nothing is stored: chips and ticks live in the URL. The rule is `service/internal/ingredient` (`Key`, `Covers`, `IsWater`), the ranking `service/internal/recipe/pantry.go`, the suggestions `service/internal/recipe/suggest.go`; the frontend is `frontend/src/routes/+page.svelte` with `SearchBar.svelte`, `PantryStamp.svelte`, `PantryFinds.svelte` and `TickRow.svelte` under `frontend/src/lib/components/recipe/`. [Recipes and search](/features/recipes) owns the list query the chips run on; [Design system](/architecture/design-system) owns the field's and the tick's look.

<Callout type="warn" title="Traps">

- `listPantry` reads every filtered recipe with `ListRecipes(…, -1, 0)`: in SQLite `LIMIT -1` is no limit, `LIMIT 0` returns nothing ([Ranking](#ranking-and-sections)).
- `ingredient.Key` never folds ä, ö, ü or ß, while the suggestions match the typed word through `db.FoldWords`, which does: "mohre" suggests "Möhre", but `?ingredients=mohre` covers nothing ([Matching](#the-matching-rule)).
- `frontend/src/lib/recipe/covers.ts` and `key.ts` are ports of `Covers` and `Key`; a change to the rule changes both sides, and `service/internal/ingredient/testdata/covers.json` is the one table both test suites read ([Matching](#the-matching-rule)).
- A tick from the URL is keyed with `ingredientKey` (`ticksNamed`) from the first render: a lower-cased stand-in ("kartoffeln" for the row's `kartoffel`) made a second, unticked pill beside the row's ([State](#state-in-the-url)).
- A canvas can measure a name a fraction narrower than the layout sets it: `longNames` compares against its room minus a margin, or a name that just misses would neither break nor fit and run out of the card ([pantry lines](#the-cards-pantry-lines)).
- The card's hover tilt moves its box by a few pixels: an e2e test that measures a card after clicking its stamp moves the mouse away first ([Testing](#testing)).
- A new list or the 300 ms skeleton mounts a fresh `RecipeGrid`: for a moment the document is short and the browser clamps `scrollY` (Chromium inside the masonry's first `layout()`, WebKit in its own layout). On a phone that dropped the search field its focus scroll had lifted, so `typingReserve` holds while the field has focus ([Focus](#focus-and-re-sort)).
- The room a held card keeps (`reserve`) is measured from the screen (`roomBelow`), never from the results' `offsetHeight`, which already includes it: added to that, every stamp stacked the same room again and the page ran on far below the last card ([Focus](#focus-and-re-sort)).
- Chromium's scroll anchoring, anchored to a card, corrects the shifts the page corrects itself and answers a line rewrapping inside a frozen card by scrolling: the results drop out of it while `frozen`. WebKit has no scroll anchoring, so only a Chromium run shows a regression ([Focus](#focus-and-re-sort)).
- `fetchPage` aborts whatever list is loading, a "Load more" too: a re-sort fetches the pages shown, so it takes the asked page from `asking`; raising `pageNum` early would put a page into the URL that has not arrived ([Re-sorting](#focus-and-re-sort)).
- `TickRow` records the hint's height only while the row is folded: taken while "+N more" stood open, it stayed after "Less" as an empty band, and the focused sheet carried it along ([Focus](#focus-and-re-sort)).
- `RecipeGrid` reads `frozen` inside its masonry attachment on purpose: the change re-attaches it, and the frozen attach measures the cards at that moment. A card's `min-height` from a freeze must go with the release, or cards keep empty space ([Focus](#focus-and-re-sort)).

</Callout>

## How it works

### The matching rule

**A chip covers an ingredient by whole key or by the head of a compound, never by prefix.** `Covers(have, need)` in `service/internal/ingredient/covers.go` takes two keys and holds when they are equal, when one is the other's last word, or when one is a single word of at least four runes that ends the other's last word. Words split on spaces and hyphens, so "Käse-Spätzle" ends in "Spätzle". "Gurke" covers "Salatgurke" and "Gewürzgurken" but not "Gurkensalat"; "Ei" covers "Eier" (same key) but not "Eigelb" or "Brei"; "olive oil" covers "oil" in reverse. The same rule serves chips, ticks ("Essig" covers "Rotweinessig") and the suggestions' "also" line. `service/internal/ingredient/testdata/covers.json` holds the cases with their keys; `covers_test.go` checks the keys against `Key` and the result against `Covers`, and `frontend/src/lib/recipe/covers.test.ts` and `key.test.ts` read the same file for the ports.

**Keys are the shopping list's.** `ingredient.Key` (`service/internal/ingredient/key.go`) lowercases, folds French and Spanish accents and singularizes the last word; [Shopping lists](/features/shopping-lists#names-and-departments) owns its rules. It lives in its own package so `recipe` and `shopping` share it without importing each other.

**Counting is once per key per recipe.** Salt in three groups counts once, group names are not ingredients, and water (`IsWater`: `wasser`, `water`) never counts as missing.

### Ranking and sections

**`ListParams.Ingredients` switches `List` to `listPantry`.** `pantryItems` trims, drops blanks and duplicate keys and caps chips at 20 and ticks at 100, silently: the API checks only a name's length (120, an ingredient's own limit), so a link with more chips never answers 422. A tick whose key equals a chip's is dropped, and so is a recipe ingredient whose key is empty. `listPantry` reads every recipe the other filters and the text let through, one `ListIngredientNamesForRecipes` for all of them, and builds each card's `CardPantry`: `chips` (how many chips it uses), `used` (its own names a chip covers) and `missing` (its other ingredients, in recipe order, with their keys). A recipe that uses no chip is left out.

**Best match ranks by chips used, then by fewest missing after the ticks.** Only `updated` ranks - what the overview's default order asks for while chips are on, named "Best match" - with a stable sort over SQL's order, so ties keep the text ranking or the recency. It fills `PagePantry.sections`, recipes counted by chips over all pages, which the overview turns into "With all 2 ingredients", "With 1 of 2 ingredients" or "With onion", each "· N found" in English, the noun the result count above them uses ("Treffer" in German). Any other order is one grid without sections. Pages are cut in Go after ranking, and only the page's cards go through `toCards` and `matchCards`, so a text search's finds still show on the cards; `matchCards` takes the names `listPantry` already read, so a request reads them once; the text sections (`Page.sections`) are never sent with chips.

**Ticks lower the rank, never the section.** `missing` on the card ignores the ticks: the client takes them off (`missingAfterTicks` in `frontend/src/lib/recipe/finds.ts`), so a tick shows on every card at once without a request. `PagePantry.row` is every ranked card's missing items in turn, once per key: the tick row. `ticked` echoes the ticks with their keys.

**The client keeps chips as the server does: one per ingredient key, at most 20.** `ingredientKey` (`frontend/src/lib/recipe/key.ts`) mirrors `ingredient.Key`, pinned by the same vectors in `service/internal/ingredient/testdata/covers.json`; change both together. `parseListQuery` drops repeats by key ("Zwiebel" and "Zwiebeln" are one chip, or "1 of 2" would read wrong) and cuts the chips at `MAX_CHIPS`, the page's `addIngredient` refuses a 21st, and at the cap the search offers no ingredient and says why above the list (`search_chip_limit`), so the suggestions' `limit` never passes the API's 50.

**A suggestion leaves the list only when it is a chip itself**, by key (`notChips` in `frontend/src/lib/recipe/ticks.ts`): "Zwiebel" stays on offer beside a chip "Frühlingszwiebeln" it covers. It reads "ticked" when a tick covers its key (`tickCovers`), compared by keys, never by the lower-cased tick name, which is not the key for a plural ("Tomaten", key `tomate`).

**The suggestions are the names in use.** `GET /api/v1/ingredients?q=` (`list-ingredients`, `recipes:read`) groups every ingredient name by key, names a group by its most frequent spelling, counts the distinct recipes using any key it covers and lists up to three other covered names as `also`. It matches `q` at the start of any word of the folded name and puts first-word matches first, then the most used.

**An empty result says what emptied it.** With a text it names the chips and the text ("No recipe with potato found for "zzz""), with filters it says the chips match none of them, and with the chips alone that no recipe contains them (`empty_pantry_text`, `empty_pantry`, `empty_pantry_none`). "Reset filters" leads when a filter is on, "Clear search" (`overview_clear_search`) when only the text is; "Remove ingredients" is always there. Each hands the focus to the search field quietly (`SearchBar`'s exported `focus`, no list, no lift), since the button unmounts with the empty state. On a coarse pointer the buttons are 44px tall (`pointer-coarse:h-11`), the `Button` default being 40.

### The card's pantry lines

**A card names what it has and what it still needs in `SearchFinds`' look** (`PantryFinds.svelte`): a rule and a label row over each line, the reader's ingredients marked like a search hit. A two-column phone card keeps the desk card's rules and label icons and drops only the label words (`sr-only`); every rule on it hangs in the card padding, so the lines keep the title's edge and full width, and the focus outline of the card the tick row works on stands off the card like the keyboard ring, so it does not double a rule. Both lines wrap in `PantryLine.svelte`, never truncated to a tooltip that touch cannot open: the reader's own ingredients hold two lines at most, the missing ones three, and each drops whole names from its end until it fits, ending in "+N" (`clampNames`, measured in an effect). The measure starts over whenever a name's parts change, not only its names: the tick row's hovered pill sets its name bold, which is wider and pushed the line past its last one, cut off.

**Only a name too wide for the room it starts in may hyphenate.** `longNames` in `frontend/src/lib/recipe/finds.ts` decides it from a canvas measure the component passes in: the first name against the room after the line's icon, every later one against a full line, its comma counted, in bold when any part of it is a hit. `FindParts` wraps only those names in `wrap-break-word hyphens-auto`, so a name that fits never breaks, the icon is never left alone on its line, and a name with no hyphenation point still breaks. The box's font and the icon's room are read in an effect once per width and per font load (`loadingdone`, since the bold face is its own file), never while rendering; the canvas and the font listener are one per page, in the component's module script, not one per card: a tick changes every card, and a layout read per card in the render path forced a layout per card. `hyphenate-limit-chars` on the box picks a better break inside such a name; current WebKit honors it and older Safari may not, and the rule does not depend on it.

### State in the URL

**Chips and ticks are `ingredients=` and `have=`, one parameter per name as picked**, never comma-joined like `tags=`: a name may hold a comma ("Milch 1,5 %"). `frontend/src/lib/recipe/query.ts` carries them beside `q` and the filters (`getAll`/`append`), and the API reads them with huma's `explode` (`query:"ingredients,explode"`). A tick writes the URL with `replaceState` (`syncUrl`), so Back does not walk through ticks; chips go through `refilter` like any filter. Ticks are sent to the API only while there are chips. Ticks from the URL get their keys from `ticksNamed` (`frontend/src/lib/recipe/ticks.ts`) through the `Key` port, so they match the row's pills before any list answers; the API's `ticked` echo is not read.

**The remembered order does not apply while there are chips.** `applyRememberedSort` (`frontend/src/lib/recipe/sort-preference.ts`) skips it when the URL has chips, and `setIngredients` switches a remembered order to best match on the first chip and gives it back when the last goes, writing nothing. An order picked during the search is remembered as usual, since `setSort` is unchanged.

**The tick row's order is frozen per filter set** (`rowFor`: chips, text, tags, time, favorites and author), so a tick, "Clear ticks" or its undo never moves a pill, while any filter that changes the cards rebuilds it from their missing items. Picking a suggestion that is ticked removes only the tick with that key: a broader tick that covers it ("Essig" for "Rotweinessig") stays.

### Focus and re-sort

**A card's stamp focuses the tick row on that card.** `focusedId` is view state, never in the URL. A phone scrolls the card to just below the sticky row, and where the screen is too short for the card down to its missing line, on until that line clears the bottom bar (`data-bottom-nav`), the card's top under the row: ticking is watching that line shrink. The focused row's pills scroll inside a height capped by a share of the screen (`dvh`), so a short screen keeps room for the card. On a phone the count and "Clear ticks" take the first-use hint's line, which is a finger's height for both, so the first tick moves neither the heading nor the pills and the button's hit area covers neither. The stamp's name carries the recipe ("Kartoffelsalat: 6 fehlen", `pantry_stamp_label`), and on a photo card it comes before the card's link in the document, lifted over the link's `::after` by `z-10`, so Tab meets it where the eye does. Activating it moves the keyboard focus to the row's first pill (or "Done" when nothing is missing) and says "Fehlt bei Kartoffelsalat, 6 Zutaten" (`ticks_focus_announce`) in a polite live region inside the tick row. "Done" follows the pills in the document and is placed back into the phone's header by grid position. Escape while a card is focused is "Done", and Done or Escape hand the focus back to the stamp (`data-stamp`); `focusCard` reads where the focus is before the row changes, since "Done" leaves the document with the focused row. "Clear ticks" goes with the ticks and the toast's undo with the toast, so both hand the focus to the general row's first pill.

**While a card is focused nothing moves: the layout is frozen,** so the card the reader ticks for stays under their eye. A tick shortens the missing line of the focused card and of others, and the masonry would put the card into another column on a phone, or a section above it would shrink on a desktop. `frozen` on the page, passed to every `RecipeGrid`, re-attaches the masonry so that each card keeps its height as `min-height` and its column; the native `grid-lanes` path gets the heights only, which is enough while ticks only shorten. `frozen` goes in the update that shows the next list, so "Done" lets go inside the re-sort's glide, not a frame before it. A desktop focus keeps the tick row's sticky box at least as tall as the general row was (`rowHeight`), or the grid would rise at the top of a page that cannot scroll up to make up for it; the sheet inside the box is only as tall as its pills, and the rest of the box lets the cards show through and take the pointer. `TickRow` keeps the height its folded row had with the first-use hint after the first tick for the same reason. The focused desktop row is a sheet over the cards: `shadow-card` in light mode, and in dark mode, which has no card shadow, `surface-elevated` with a `border` edge. Focusing cancels a pending re-sort and aborts a quiet one in flight. `refilter`, a text change and `followUrl` release the focus, and the list they asked for releases a card a stamp focused on the old cards while it loaded (`show` in `fetchPage`), since it arrives unfrozen, and hands the keyboard back to that card's stamp, or to the search field when the card is gone.

**The page holds the card, the browser does not.** As a backstop an `$effect` holds the focused card's place on the page after every `style` write of the masonry, and every animation frame while something may move it: a tap, a key or a size change of the tick row or the results starts the frames, and a second without a move stops them, so a focus left open reads no layout. While `frozen` the results carry `overflow-anchor: none`, so Chromium's scroll anchoring never anchors to a card: it would make up a second time for the shifts `focusCard` corrects, such as the row's new height on "Done", and it would scroll the page when a missing line rewraps inside a frozen card, which the hold cannot see, since a scroll leaves a card's place on the page alone.

**Re-sorting waits.** Without a focused card, a quiet re-fetch runs 0.9 s after the last tick: no skeleton, and the cards glide (`glide`). A tick aborts a quiet re-fetch still under way, and `show` drops an answer whose request a focus aborted while its glide waited for a frame, so cards never glide under a tick or a focus. "Load more" sends the ticks the cards on screen were ranked by (`rankedHave`), not the newest, so the next page continues that ranking, and it leaves a pending re-sort to run. A re-sort aborts a "Load more" still loading and fetches its page along: `loadMore` notes the page it asks for (`asking`) and a quiet fetch reads it, while `pageNum`, which the URL names, counts only the pages shown. The other way round cannot happen, since the button is disabled while any list loads. "Done" re-fetches with the released card as the glide's anchor, so it stays where it is while the others move, or glides down with the page's end where staying would take room below the last card.

**A held card gets room under the screen, and only while it is held.** At a page's end the browser clamps the scroll: the phone's scroll up to a focused card would stop short, and a row that changes height or a re-sort that shortens the page would pull the pinned card down. So every stamp and "Done" first let the results reach a fixed slack past the screen's bottom (`reserve` through `roomBelow`), and the phone's scroll to the card raises it to exactly what that scroll needs. The next list lets go of the room in the update it glides to (`show`): the released card stays where it is while the list reaches past the screen, and where it does not, the page's end comes up in that glide and the card glides down with it, so no band of nothing stays under the cards and nothing trims the room on a scroll. A re-sort that fails lets go of the held layout and the room in a glide of its own (`catch` in `fetchPage`), for the same reason. On a phone `typingReserve` holds the results' height while the search field has focus, across query changes: `fetchPage` raises it when the fetch starts, before the skeleton or the answer swaps the grids, and it goes when the focus leaves the search row. It is kept apart from `reserve`, so leaving the field never takes a card's room with it; the results keep the larger of the two.

### Testing

**`describe('ingredient search')` in `frontend/e2e/recipes.test.ts` covers the flows** on German fixture recipes tagged per test, so parallel tests stay out of the grid. Tests that measure a card after a stamp click move the mouse away and wait, because the hover tilt shifts the box. "a re-sort the ticks started waits while a card is focused" counts the list requests: the focused card's place cannot show a re-sort, since the hold keeps the card still while the list re-sorts under it. "a focused card holds still" ticks every pill of a long card on a phone and on a wide desktop and checks x and y each time; the demo reproduces the same with `/?ingredients=Kartoffeln&ingredients=Zwiebel`. "a name too long for the small phone card" holds the two-column card on the smallest phone, where "Schweineschnitzel" would run out of the missing line without its hyphenation. "a name the hover sets bold" narrows every missing line and spaces out the bold name with an injected style, since the sample names leave the desk lines too much room for bold alone to overflow. Clicking "+N more" needs a DOM, which the unit project lacks; e2e covers it. Below `md` the row shows every pill, so "+N more" is `max-md:hidden`; written `hidden` with `md:inline-flex`, it lost to the `inline-flex` in the shared link classes and showed on every phone. "a mouse moves the narrow row by its arrows" drives the phone row in a narrow desktop window with a mouse ([Scrolling](/architecture/design-system#scrolling)).

**The room and "Done" are tested where Safari and Chromium differ.** "focused cards never stretch the page past the list" taps four stamps, scrolls to the page's end and taps "Done": the page then ends at its own margin under the last card. "a card let go of with Done holds its place, or glides down with the end" lets go of two cards in the list, which hold still, and of the last card from the page's end, which may only go down, and a card that moves must show up on the way in some frame of its glide (its `::view-transition-group`'s `transform`, sampled every frame), or it jumped. Where the re-sort puts cards below the last one, as on the desktop project, it holds and that check has nothing to catch. "a card let go of with Done glides down even when its re-sort fails" answers the re-sort with a 500 and asks the same of the failure's own glide, where no re-sort reorders the cards. Its Sauerbraten and Maultaschen and the Käsespätzle case of "a focused card holds still" are the cards Chromium anchors to in that layout; another card may hold by chance. These three also run on the `webkit-masonry` project, and they wait on conditions (`still`, `done`), not on time. "Done while more cards load keeps the page Load more asked for" holds the second page's answer back and taps "Done" meanwhile; "the address names only the pages on screen while more load" ticks while it loads and then fails it.

**A slow renderer must not fail these tests.** WebKit on Linux can hold the 450ms glide's first frame for 400ms and paint nothing between its two ends, so a card also counts as glided when its group's animation starts where the card was and runs for a while. The failure's toast lasts four seconds, which such a glide can outlast: the test watches for it from "Done" on. Ticks, settles and page loads add up past the 30s default on CI's four shared cores, so "holds still" and "never cuts its placeholder" set their own timeout. The WebKit phone projects run at one device pixel per CSS pixel: at the iPhone's 3x, Linux WebKit's CPU repaint of the cards' blurred shadows took a second a frame there, and every click waits two frames ([Testing](/conventions/testing)).

## Why it is this way

**Inside the overview search, not a page of its own.** "Where is rum used?" and "what can I cook with these?" stay one path, the filters exist once, and the menu gets no further item.

**Whole-word and compound-head matching**, because German compounds put the thing at the end: a prefix match would make "Ei" find "Eigelb" and "Rum" find "Rumpsteak". The accepted price is "Zwiebel" covering "Frühlingszwiebeln", so a card always names the recipe's own ingredient, never the chip.

**Most chips first, fewest missing second.** The recipe that uses all of the reader's leftovers belongs on top; fewest-missing first rewards short recipes.

**Ticks instead of a stored pantry.** People who share an instance do not share a kitchen, and a pantry list goes stale; ticking what is at home costs a tap and is gone with the URL.

**Ranking in Go over all filtered recipes** is a few milliseconds at a small instance's scale and keeps the rule in one function; the `ponytail:` comment on `listPantry` names the ceiling.

**"Done" lets the card glide down rather than leave room.** Holding the released card at the page's end needs room below the last card, which reads as empty space under the cards; gliding down with the page's end shows where the card went.

## Rejected

- **A page of its own ("What do I have?").** A further menu item, the filters built twice, and two ways to answer "where is rum?".
- **A "Search / By ingredient" switch on the overview.** It costs a click, and "gurke" typed in the normal mode lands in the old search.
- **Fewest missing first.** It rewards short recipes and buries the one that uses everything.
- **A staples list,** fixed in code, per instance or per person: the reader ticks as needed instead.
- **Ticking in a popover from the card,** an expanding card, or tapping words on the card: the opener was invisible, the popover covered content, the grid jumped, and targets under 44 px sat inside the card's link.
- **The plain wheel moving the narrow row sideways.** The row is sticky, so a page scroll with the pointer over it moved the pills first, for dozens of notches; shift and the wheel or a trackpad's sideways swipe move it natively ([Scrolling](/architecture/design-system#scrolling)).
- **Only one global tick row ordered by count.** On a phone one recipe's vinegar sat screens away; the focused mode answers that.
- **Keeping the released card in place at the page's end** with the room under it, cut back to the screen's bottom and then as the reader scrolled: a band of nothing stayed in view after "Done".
- **Svelte's `animate:flip` for the re-sort.** The cards are a component inside the keyed each, and the masonry writes `left`/`top` from observers, which FLIP's measurements do not line up with; `glide` already moves them.

## Limits

- No synonyms, substitutions, food database or cross-language matching: "Gurke" never finds "cucumber".
- No optional ingredients; every ingredient but water can be missing.
- Suffix matching can over-match ("Reis" and "Preis"), and plurals beyond `Key`'s rules stay separate keys.
- The command palette does not offer ingredients.
