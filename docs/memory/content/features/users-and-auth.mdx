Several people in a household share one recipe collection. Accounts are created, re-roled, reset and removed in the UI; only the instance owner comes from the environment. Every account carries a display name and a palette color on top of its login name, which together are how the UI names and marks a person; [Design system](/architecture/design-system) owns the tokens the color resolves to. The served OpenAPI document holds the endpoints, their bodies and their status codes ([HTTP API](/architecture/api)); [Data model](/architecture/data-model) owns the `users` and `sessions` tables.

## How it works

### Login and sessions

**A login form, and argon2id underneath it.** `frontend/src/routes/login/+page.svelte` posts username and password to the login operation; `user.Service.Authenticate` trims the username the way `Create` trims it and runs `VerifyPassword`. `HashPassword` in `service/internal/user/password.go` produces a PHC string (`$argon2id$…`) with 64 MiB of memory, one iteration, four lanes, a 16-byte salt and a 32-byte key. `VerifyPassword` reads those parameters back out of the stored hash and bounds each of them — at most 1 GiB, 16 iterations, 32 lanes — so a tampered row cannot make one login attempt allocate a gigabyte. An unknown username is still charged one argon2 evaluation, against a `dummyHash` computed once per process, so timing does not separate "no such user" from "wrong password".

**A name that keeps failing is locked.** `auth.Service.Login` asks the throttle in `service/internal/auth/throttle.go` before it touches the password.

- It counts attempts per username, folded the way the `users` column compares it: trimmed like `Authenticate` trims it, ASCII letters lowercased and every other letter left alone, which is what `COLLATE NOCASE` does.
- Five attempts are free, the fifth arms a 30-second lock, and every attempt after a lock has run out doubles it, up to 15 minutes. A locked name gets `*auth.ThrottledError` at once, which the handler turns into `429` with `Retry-After` in whole seconds, rounded up; the SPA shows `login_throttled`.
- A successful login forgets the name, and so does a day without attempts, swept on the same tick as expired sessions.

**Argon2 runs four at a time.** `deriveKey` in `service/internal/user/password.go` runs at most `maxConcurrentArgon` (4) derivations at once - logins, password changes and new hashes alike - and lets at most `maxQueuedArgon` (32) more wait for a slot. A waiter whose request context ends gives up its place. A caller that finds the queue full gets `user.ErrBusy`, which `auth.BusyError` turns into `503` with `Retry-After: 1` on every operation that hashes or checks a password. `auth.DeclareRetryAfter` puts that header into the OpenAPI document too, because huma writes error responses from an operation's `Errors` list without headers.

**The token lives in the cookie, its digest in the table.** `Login` in `service/internal/auth/service.go` draws 32 random bytes, encodes them with `base64.RawURLEncoding` and stores only the SHA-256 of that string as the session id, so a database leak yields no usable session. The token goes back as the `rezepte_session` cookie: `HttpOnly`, `Path=/`, `SameSite=Lax`, and `Secure` when `REZEPTE_SECURE_COOKIES` is set.

`SessionTTL` is 30 days and slides: `Authenticate` resets it to a full 30 days once less than `SessionTTL - renewAfter` is left, with `renewAfter` at 24 hours, so an active session is written back at most once a day. The renewal is reported as `Verified.Renewed`, and both callers re-issue the cookie with the fresh expiry so the browser's copy never falls behind the row. A session found past its expiry is deleted on the spot; `DeleteExpired` also runs once at startup and `SweepLoop` every 24 hours after that.

**One middleware, two authenticators.** `auth.Middleware` authenticates every operation that declares `Security` — `auth.SessionSecurity` for session-only operations, `auth.Protected(scopes...)` for the ones an API token may also reach; an operation without `Security` is public. It is installed through `httpserver.WithAPIMiddleware`, and `httpserver.New` applies every `Option` before it returns — so there is no window in which an operation could be registered ahead of it. A request carrying an `Authorization: Bearer` header is decided by a branch inside `Middleware` alone, with no fallback to the cookie; [API tokens](/features/api-tokens) owns its outcomes. `auth.TokenOnly(scopes...)` is the third kind of `Security`, for an operation no session may reach. The contract routes are the only routes that are not operations and still need authentication; see [Packages and the contract routes](#packages-and-the-contract-routes).

**CSRF is `SameSite=Lax` plus an origin check.** `checkOrigin` in `service/internal/httpserver/origin.go` refuses a `POST`, `PUT`, `PATCH` or `DELETE` under `/api/`, and a request of any method to `/mcp`, whose `Origin` header names a host other than the request's (or cannot be parsed). `/mcp` is covered whatever the method because the MCP Streamable HTTP transport requires every server to validate `Origin` and answer a present, invalid one with `403`. A request that carries no `Origin` passes: that is a non-browser client, which the cookie's `SameSite` does not constrain either. [HTTP API](/architecture/api) has the response body.

### Roles and rank

**Three ranked roles, compared in one file.** `user.RoleUser`, `user.RoleAdmin` and `user.RoleSuperadmin` are the whole set, in ascending rank. The superadmin is the instance owner and is an admin.

- **Ask the role, never compare it.** `Role.IsAdmin` and `Role.IsSuperadmin` in `service/internal/user/service.go` are the only way to ask what a role carries: a bare comparison against `RoleAdmin` excludes the owner and is always a bug. The SPA has the same trap and the same answer: `isAdminRole` in `frontend/src/lib/roles.ts`, because `role === 'admin'` locks the owner out of every admin surface. `roleLabel` (the only place a role becomes label text) and `roleRank` (the people list's order) sit in that module too.
- **`requireAdmin`** in `service/internal/userapi/handler.go` answers `401` without an authenticated user and `403` `admin role required` when `IsAdmin` is false. Every user management operation calls it first; update and delete pass the caller it returns into the service.
- **Everything finer lives in `service/internal/user/permission.go` and nowhere else.** `guardTarget` says who may act on whom and, applied to a role instead of a row, who may hand out which role. `CanAssignRole` is that second use, exported for the create path, which has a requested role but no target row.
- **The whole rule:** an admin manages plain users; anything touching an admin or the superadmin belongs to the superadmin; and the superadmin can be neither deleted nor demoted by anyone, themselves included. There is no permission table. Recipes carry the one per-resource rule, which [Editing rights](/features/editing-rights) owns.
- **A token acts as its issuer's account**, so the rank applies underneath its scopes: a `users:write` token issued by an admin who is not the owner cannot hand out `admin` any more than that admin can. [API tokens](/features/api-tokens) owns how a token re-reads its issuer.

**The matrix, once, so it need not be re-derived.** Rows are the caller, columns the target; a cell says what the API answers. Creation has no target row, so it is `CanAssignRole` alone: an admin may ask for `user`, only the owner may ask for `admin`, and `superadmin` is refused by huma's `enum:"admin,user"` with `422` before a handler runs.

| Caller | Target: a member | Target: an admin | Target: the owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| member | `403` `admin role required`, from `requireAdmin` before rank is consulted | same | same |
| admin | delete, reset, a setup link, setting the email address and the public-sharing switch; a role change and a profile write are each `403` `superadmin role required` | `403` `superadmin role required` | `409` `the superadmin cannot be modified` |
| the owner | delete, reset, re-role, rename, recolor, set the email address and the public-sharing switch | delete, reset, re-role, rename, recolor, set the email address and the public-sharing switch | `409` `the superadmin cannot be modified` for delete, reset, setting the email address, re-role and the public-sharing switch; a profile write lands |

- The profile write is the one column that never reads the target's rank: `guardProfileEdit` asks only whether the caller is the owner, so the owner reaches every row including their own, and everybody else is `403` `superadmin role required` whoever they aim at. The people list still hides it on the caller's own row: the profile page is the one place a person renames themselves.
- Nobody deletes their own account (`409` `cannot delete yourself`, checked before the transaction opens), and nobody resets their own password through the admin path — an admin aiming at their own row is aiming at an admin.
- `docs/user/content/guide/users.mdx` states the same matrix as what a person sees in the row, since an action the caller may not take is never rendered.

**The rank guard runs inside the write transaction.**

- `user.Service.Delete` refuses `actor.ID == id` with `ErrSelfDelete`, a plain comparison before any transaction opens. Everything else is `guardTarget`, which `SetRole`, `Delete` and `SetPassword` run on the row they have just read, inside the write transaction `db.Tx` opens. It takes its write lock at `BEGIN` because of the connection DSN [Data model](/architecture/data-model) lists, so a concurrent promotion cannot slip the target out from under a check that has already passed. `SetRole` runs `guardTarget` a second time, on the role it is about to write.
- `SetPassword` hashes before opening the transaction: holding the `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` lock for an argon2id run would block every other writer for the length of it.
- Each refusal carries one status code. `ErrSuperadminProtected` is `409` `the superadmin cannot be modified`, a fact about the instance that holds for every caller, in the register `ErrSelfDelete` uses; `ErrSuperadminRequired` is `403` `superadmin role required`, which is about who is asking.
- Two SQLite triggers, `users_superadmin_undeletable` and `users_superadmin_role_locked`, refuse the delete and the demotion underneath the Go layer, for a raw query or a code path that forgets to ask; [Data model](/architecture/data-model) owns them.

**Deleting a user hands their recipes to the acting admin.** `ReassignRecipes` moves the recipes they created **or last edited** to the acting admin (the token's issuer when the call carried a bearer token, since `requireAdmin` reads whichever account the credential belongs to), in the same transaction. `created_by` and `updated_by` both reference `users` with no `ON DELETE` clause, so a recipe somebody else wrote but this user last touched would otherwise hold the delete on its foreign key. `edit_policy` stays as it is, so each recipe keeps its [editing rights](/features/editing-rights) under its new author. Their [comments](/features/recipe-comments) are not reassigned: `author_id` is `ON DELETE SET NULL`, so they stay and read "Former member", since words a person wrote must not become the admin's; their watermarks go. Their sessions go with the row (see [Data model](/architecture/data-model)).

**The bootstrap account is the owner, and only a shell resets it.** `EnsureSuperadmin` runs as the process starts and has three answers:

- an empty instance gets the account `REZEPTE_ADMIN_USER` names, created with `RoleSuperadmin` through `Create`, so it picks up the same profile defaults as every other account: its display name is its login name, and its color is `amber`, which is what the least-used rule yields on an empty table (every count zero, the tie going to palette order);
- an instance that already has an owner is left alone;
- a populated `users` table with no owner is `ErrNoSuperadmin`, which stops the service from coming up rather than let it serve an instance nobody owns.

`superadmin` is never an assignable role — `POST /users` and `PATCH /users/{id}` declare `enum:"admin,user"` — and the owner's password is theirs alone: the admin reset refuses that target for every caller, the owner included, and self-service goes through `ChangePassword`. The one way back from a forgotten owner password is outside the application: `rezepte --reset-superadmin-password` takes the new password from `REZEPTE_ADMIN_PASSWORD`, writes it to the owner, ends every session of that account and returns without starting the server. Reaching it needs shell access to the host, which is the operator by definition.

### Profile: name, color, picture, address

**Every account carries a display name and a color.** `display_name` is what the UI prints wherever it names somebody and `color` is the palette token that marks them; neither is an identity and neither is unique - `username` stays the only thing that identifies an account.

- `user.Create` is the single writer of a user row, so it owns both defaults: an empty display name becomes the trimmed username, and an empty color becomes the least-used color of the palette, ties broken by palette order (`leastUsed` in `service/internal/user/profile.go`, over the counts `ColorUsage` groups out of the table). Least-used rather than first-free because duplicates are allowed: the two only part company once every color is held, and spreading a household across the palette is the property worth keeping.
- `normalizeDisplayName` trims, falls back to the username when nothing printable is left, refuses more than 64 **runes** and refuses a control character - so the column is never empty and no client invents a fallback of its own.
- `ParseColor` accepts the palette exactly as written, with no trimming and no case folding: the API declares the closed set as an `enum`, so anything else is a client error rather than a typo to repair.
- Self-service is `PATCH /api/v1/auth/me/profile`, which every role may use on its own row; `GET /api/v1/auth/me/colors` gives the picker a count per color - counts only, no names. Writing somebody **else's** profile is the owner's alone: `user.CanEditProfile` in `service/internal/user/permission.go`, checked in `update-user` before anything is written, so a body that mixes a rename with a password reset changes neither.

**Every account may carry a picture.** `users.avatar_id` names it, `NULL` means none, and a circle without one is the color and initial.

- The account sets and removes its own through `PUT`/`DELETE /api/v1/auth/me/avatar`; the owner does both for any account through `/api/v1/users/{id}/avatar`, the rule `user.CanEditProfile` applies to the rest of a profile.
- `service/internal/avatar` stores one square JPEG per account at `<REZEPTE_DATA_DIR>/avatars/<user id>/<avatar id>.jpg`, rendered by package image ([Images](/features/images) owns the pixels), with a fresh `avatar_id` per upload, so a changed picture is a changed URL and the file is cached for a year.
- `Service.Set` renders the file first, swaps the row in a transaction, and removes the previous file only once the row names the new one; a failed swap removes the new file. The row never names a missing file, and a failure leaves the old picture in place. `Remove` clears the column and deletes the file, and removing a picture that is not there is not an error. `delete-user` calls `RemoveAll` after the row is gone.
- Every session, and a token with `users:read`, may read the files; the OpenAPI document has the routes and [Design system](/architecture/design-system) the circle.

**An account may carry an email address, and it is profile data, not an identity.** `email` is not unique and plays no part in signing in.

- It is set through the same `PATCH /api/v1/auth/me/profile` as name and color; `ParseEmail` in `service/internal/user/email.go` accepts only a bare address (no display name, no angle brackets) and treats `""` as "none".
- `email_verified` is true when an identity provider vouched for the address through its own `email_verified` claim ([OIDC](/features/oidc)), when a setup link mailed to it was redeemed, or when its confirmation link was opened ([Email](/features/email)). Typing a new address clears it; the same address in other letters is the same mailbox, so `nextEmail` keeps the stored spelling and the mark and nothing is mailed.
- `(*user.Service).SetEmailIfEmpty` is the other writer: it fills the column only while it is empty, so a provider's address becomes the account's own on the first sign-in through it and is left alone afterwards - a typed address always outranks a provider's.
- `userapi.UserAccount` (`GET /api/v1/users`, admins only) carries `email` and `emailVerified`; `PersonEntry`, the people list every account reads, carries neither, so a member never sees another member's address.
- An admin writes another account's address through `PATCH /api/v1/users/{id}` (`email`) under `user.CanManage`, like a reset: `(*user.Service).SetEmail` runs `guardTarget` inside the write transaction, and a new address clears `email_verified`. That write is session-only - a token gets `422` at `body.email` - since an address decides where a credential is mailed.
- Creating an account with an address needs only `CanAssignRole`, and a token may do it: a token-created account carries a password, so no setup link goes to that address.
- In the people list that write is the address field of the row's pencil, `EditProfileDialog.svelte`, offered when `rowPermissions` grants `editProfile` or `manageAccount`. It renders photo, name and color under `editProfile` and the address under `manageAccount`, and sends only the fields that changed, so a plain admin on a member's row gets and sends the address alone and never trips the owner-only profile write.

**An account carries whether it may share publicly.** `users.can_share_publicly` is an admin's per-person switch, on by default, returned as `canSharePublicly` in `UserAccount`. `PATCH /users/{id}` accepts it under `guardTarget` like a reset: an admin switches it for plain members, only the owner for admins, and the owner's own row always answers `409` `the superadmin cannot be modified`, since the owner's control is the household-wide switch, not a per-person right. One rank rule for the role change, the reset and this switch is what lets `update-user` take them in one body without one landing while another is refused; an admin aiming at their own row is aiming at an admin, so nobody re-grants themselves a right another admin withdrew. In the SPA, `rowPermissions` grants `toggleSharing` exactly where it grants `manageAccount`. [Public shares](/features/public-shares) owns what withdrawing does to existing links.

### The people list

**Every account sees who takes part.** `GET /api/v1/people` (`list-people`, in `userapi`) answers any session with every account as a `PersonEntry` - `id`, `username`, `displayName`, `color`, `avatarId`, `role` - and nothing else. It is the one operation in the package that does not call `requireAdmin`. `TestPeopleHidesAccountDetails` pins its key set field by field, so a field added to the struct has to be added to the test on purpose, and `TestPeopleNeedsTheUsersReadScope` holds tokens to `users:read`. The frontend type is `PersonEntry` in `frontend/src/lib/api/users.ts` too, because `Person` in `frontend/src/lib/api/recipes.ts` is the roleless person a recipe names, and two exports called `Person` would be one wrong import apart.

**One page for every rank.** Settings → People (`frontend/src/routes/settings/users/+page.svelte`; the path says `users` because accounts are managed there) reads `/people` for every viewer; admins also read `GET /users` for what only they may see (address, setup state, `canSharePublicly`). What differs by rank is what surrounds the list, decided per row by `rowPermissions` in `frontend/src/lib/settings/row-permissions.ts`. That function asks the viewer's rank first, because the page is open to every account, then mirrors `permission.go`; `PersonRow.svelte` and, on a phone, `PersonSheet.svelte` only render what it returns.

**The people list decides its own order.** `ListUsers` is a plain `ORDER BY username` under the column's `COLLATE NOCASE`, which is case-insensitive across ASCII only and files "Änna" behind "Zoe"; that is the API's contract and stays as it is. The page orders on the client: `groupPeople` in `frontend/src/lib/settings/people-groups.ts` makes one group per role somebody holds, in `roleRank` order, and orders each group by display name with an `Intl.Collator` over Paraglide's `getLocale()`.

- The order must stay total: a collator tie falls through to a code-point comparison, then to the username (`compareUsernames`, which does the same), then to the id. `numeric: true` calls "user2" and "user02" equal, and a stable sort would then keep whatever order the rows arrived in, so a row would move between loads.
- `PeopleList.svelte` derives the groups from the list it is handed, so a new account lands where it will sit after a reload and a promoted one moves under "Admins" when the change is saved.

**What a row says and offers.** Under a person's name, for an admin who may manage that account, `PersonRow` shows the account's setup state: "Setup link open until" an open link's expiry, "Not set up yet" for an account with neither a password, a connected identity nor an open link, and `Signs in with {name}` for one connected to the instance's provider (its `hasIdentity` comes from [OIDC](/features/oidc)). An account with both an identity and an open link shows both lines.

- The rarely touched account actions - setup link, reset, revoke link, disconnect, "May share publicly", delete - share one row-scoped `DropdownMenu` on a wide screen, so the row fits the settings card; the role select and the pencil, touched often, stay outside it. On a phone they are `PersonSheet.svelte`'s rows instead.
- On a phone the whole row is one tap target, so the status lines are text only on every screen: a button inside the row would be a second target under the same finger.
- Each menu item keeps an accessible name that says whose account it acts on, since nothing else on the page does.
- Focus is put back by id (`frontend/src/lib/settings/person-focus.ts`): a role change re-creates the row in its new group, and a menu or sheet that opens a dialog first hands focus to its own trigger, so the dialog has a stable element to return it to. Deleting removes the row, so the card's heading (`tabindex="-1"`) takes focus instead of `<body>`.
- A role takes effect with one tap, so its toast names the new role and carries "Undo", which sends the previous role back through the same path.

**Three words, three things.** *User* and *account* name the login and its management: `/api/v1/users`, the `users:*` scopes, "Add account". *Person* and *people* name who takes part in the household: `/api/v1/people`, the `Person` a recipe's `createdBy` carries, the "People" tab. *Member* names one role, `user`, and nothing else. The German catalog draws the same line ("Benutzer" for the account, "Mitglied" for the role), so no label in either language can be read as both.

### Setup links, reset links and `/welcome`

**Setup links instead of handed-over passwords.** Creating a user with no `password` (`POST /api/v1/users`, `createInput.Body.Password` is `omitempty`) stores an empty `password_hash` and issues a setup link in the same response.

- `auth.Service.IssueSetupLink` draws 32 random bytes the way a session token is drawn, stores only `hashToken` of it in `setup_links` (`0010_setup_links.sql`), and returns the raw token once.
- `setup_links` holds one row per account (`setup_links_user_idx` is unique on `user_id`), so `ReplaceSetupLink`'s `ON CONFLICT (user_id) DO UPDATE` is the whole of "issuing another replaces it": there is never a second open link, and the old token stops resolving the moment a new one is stored.
- `auth.SetupPath` puts the token in the URL fragment, `"/welcome#" + token`: a fragment never leaves the browser, so it reaches neither a server log nor a `Referer` header. `auth.SetupLinkTTL` is 7 days, swept by `DeleteExpired` with expired sessions.
- Issuing a link is the same act as a password reset and follows the same rank rule: `user.CanManage` is `guardTarget` under a name that reads right for "may hand this account a fresh credential", called from `IssueSetupLink` and from `RevokeSetupLink`. A revoke deletes the row if one exists and is not an error when none does.
- Every setup operation is session-only (`auth.SessionSecurity`): issuing a credential is not a thing a `users:write` token should do unattended.
- A token cannot create a user with an empty `password` either, since redeeming a setup link takes the admin UI's dialog, which a token cannot show: `create-user` answers `422` at `body.password`, deciding "bearer caller" with `auth.ViaToken(ctx)`, a context flag only `auth.Middleware`'s bearer branch sets.
- `GET /api/v1/users` calls `sessions.OpenSetupLinks` once per request and folds `setupLinkExpiresAt` into each row with an open link, so the list needs no request per row; `hasPassword` rides on every row, and `auth.UserResponse` carries it too.

**Redeeming is the public half, and spends a link exactly once.**

- `POST /api/v1/auth/setup/inspect` (`PeekSetupLink`) answers a token with the username and display name to greet, without spending it.
- `POST /api/v1/auth/setup/password` (`RedeemWithPassword`) hashes the new password **before** touching the link, so a full argon2 queue (`user.ErrBusy` → `503`) leaves the link open for a retry. It then runs one transaction: the delete-returning consume (`consumeWith`, the one place both `ConsumeSetupLink` and `RedeemWithPassword` map a spent or unknown token to `ErrNoSetupLink`), the password write, and `DeleteUserSessionsExcept` with an empty keep-token, since an invite is not assumed to be the person's only browser.
- A concurrent second redemption finds no row left to delete, so of two requests racing on one link exactly one sets a password (`TestRedeemSetupLinkOnlyOnce`, under `-race`).
- Unknown, expired and used all answer the same `ErrNoSetupLink` → `404`, so a probe learns nothing about which case it hit.
- Success signs the account in through `StartSession`, the call `Login` uses, so accepting an invite and being signed in are one step.

**A forgotten password is reset through a link of its own.** A reset link is a `setup_links` row with `purpose = 'reset'` (`auth.PurposeReset`), issued by `IssueResetLink` for one hour (`ResetLinkTTL`) with `created_by` the account itself.

- It replaces an older or expired reset link, never an open setup link an admin handed out: `ReplaceSetupLink`'s upsert updates no row then, `issueLink` answers `errSetupLinkOpen`, and the request mails nothing.
- Changing the address or the password closes it: the trigger `users_change_closes_reset_link` deletes the account's `purpose = 'reset'` row on every write of `email` (case folded) or `password_hash`, whichever code path makes it, and leaves a setup link alone.
- The people list's "setup link open" line (`ListOpenSetupLinks`) counts only `purpose = 'setup'`, so a member's own reset request never shows there.
- It opens `/welcome` in reset mode, which names the account, offers no identity provider button and ends every other session when the password is saved. `PeekSetupLinkByHash`, the OIDC flow's peek, refuses a reset link, so a hand-made `intent=setup` post can never attach an identity to the account.
- The owner is never reached by it. Two cooldowns sit next to the login throttle: one reset mail per account per five minutes and one confirmation mail per account per minute; [Email](/features/email) owns who gets what.

**Issuing a link in the UI.** "Add account" (`CreateUserDialog.svelte`) defaults to "Send a setup link"; a link-mode create opens `SetupLinkDialog.svelte` with the token, which leads with where it was mailed when it was ([Email](/features/email)) and always shows the link itself as the fallback. A row's "Setup link" action, under the `manageAccount` rule, opens the same dialog at any later point and replaces whatever link was open; with mail on it first asks in `SendSetupLinkDialog.svelte` whether to mail the link or only show it.

**`/welcome` is where a link lands.** `frontend/src/routes/welcome/+page.svelte` reads the token from `location.hash` once, on mount, and immediately removes it from the address bar with `history.replaceState` - whether or not the form is ever finished - so an abandoned invite never sits as a live token in browser history. It calls `inspectSetupLink` to greet the person and `redeemSetupLink` on submit. A `404` from either is the only case that shows "This link no longer works"; any other inspect failure offers a retry, since the token stays in the page's memory. `/welcome` is exempt from the root layout's login redirect, so a browser already signed in as another account shows a notice naming both, and finishing the link replaces that session.

### Passwords

**A member may change their own password.** That and the own profile are the two user writes a non-admin can do, and both sit next to `me` rather than on the admin resource. `user.Service.ChangePassword` verifies the current password before storing the new one; a mismatch is `user.ErrWrongPassword`, located at `body.currentPassword`.

**An account may have no password.** `password_hash` is `''`, not `NULL` - a bare `TEXT NOT NULL` needs no migration to add the state, and every reader already treats the column as a string.

- `user.Create` stores the empty hash whenever `CreateParams.Password` is empty, except for the superadmin: `Create` refuses `RoleSuperadmin` without a password (`ErrAdminPasswordRequired`), so the instance's own way back in always has one.
- `Authenticate` treats an empty hash exactly like a wrong password and still runs one `VerifyPassword` against `dummyHash`, so neither timing nor response tells a caller a name exists with no password to guess.
- `ChangePassword` skips the current-password check while there is none: the caller's own session is proof enough, so `PATCH /api/v1/auth/me` accepts a body with no `currentPassword`. Once a password is set, the next change demands it like any other.

**Every password change revokes sessions, and the two kinds differ only in what they keep.** Both go through `auth.Service.DeleteUserSessionsExcept`. The own change passes the calling cookie's token, so the browser making the change stays signed in and every other device is signed out. The admin reset passes an empty token, which keeps nothing: a reset means the old credential is forgotten or compromised.

**Every password field is written for a password manager.** A manager fills and saves by `autocomplete` alone.

- The login form marks its fields `username` and `current-password`; every field that sets a password is `new-password`, which also makes a browser offer a generated one. The login's username field turns off `autocapitalize` and `spellcheck`, because a phone keyboard that capitalizes `sam` to `Sam` fails the login.
- `PasswordForm.svelte` has no visible username, so it carries a hidden, read-only `autocomplete="username"` field holding the caller's own: without it a manager cannot tell which account the password belongs to and saves a second entry. The two admin dialogs stay without one on purpose, because the password they set belongs to somebody else.
- The login's username field carries `autofocus`, and it has to be the attribute rather than a `focus()` call on mount: SvelteKit's `reset_focus` moves focus to `<body>` after every navigation, the redirect to `/login` included, and spares only an element marked `autofocus`.

**A new password is rated while it is typed, and only rated.** `PasswordStrength.svelte` sits under the field in all three forms that set a password - the own change, "Add account" and the admin reset. `estimateStrength` in `frontend/src/lib/settings/password-strength.ts` runs `@zxcvbn-ts/core` with `@zxcvbn-ts/language-common` (the leaked-password list and the keyboard graphs), both behind a dynamic `import()`, so the roughly 230 KB gzipped reach a browser only when somebody types into one of those fields, never on the login or a recipe page. It passes the account's username and display name, whole and word by word, plus `rezepte` and `recipes`, so `anna1990` is weak for Anna and nobody else. The rating waits 250 ms after the last keystroke and is a polite live region. Nothing is blocked: the length rule the API enforces is the only rule a password must pass.

**Every form that sets a password takes it twice and states the minimum.** `validateNewPassword` in `frontend/src/lib/settings/password.ts` checks the pair before anything is sent; the API never sees the second copy. The new-password field's hint, "At least 8 characters", is the same message the too-short error uses, so a failed save turns the hint red in place instead of adding a line.

### Packages and the contract routes

**User management and token management are their own packages because of the import direction.** `auth` imports `user`, and both the user handlers and the token handlers need `auth.UserFrom` and `auth.SessionSecurity` or `auth.Protected`. Registering either from `user`, or from `auth` itself, would close the cycle, so they live in `userapi` and `tokenapi`.

**The contract routes require a session or a token.** `isSpecRoute` in `service/internal/httpserver/api.go` matches `/api/v1/docs`, everything under the `/api/v1/openapi` prefix and everything under `/api/v1/schemas/`. huma hangs those off the mux rather than registering them as operations, so `auth.Middleware` never sees them; `httpserver.WithSpecGuard` wraps them in front of the mux instead, and `service/cmd/rezepte/main.go` supplies `auth.RequireAuthOrLogin` from `service/internal/auth/require.go` - plain `func(http.Handler) http.Handler` middleware with the same session authentication and renewal re-issue. Any role is enough, and a valid API token of any scope opens them too, since `RequireAuthOrLogin` checks no scope. A `Server` built without the option serves them openly, so the package stays unopinionated. Matching by prefix costs one rule: no operation may ever be registered at a path under those prefixes.

**A browser is redirected, everything else gets a `401`.** A bearer request is never redirected: a bad token gets a plain `401`, because a login page means nothing to a client that authenticates with a header. For a session, `navigatingBrowser` accepts a `GET` whose `Accept` contains `text/html` and answers it with `303` to `/login?next=<escaped request URI>`; a bare `*/*`, which `fetch` and curl send, does not count, and neither does any other method, so those keep the `401` problem body. The SPA closes the loop: `safeNext` in `frontend/src/lib/api/auth.ts` resolves `next` against a fixed fake origin and falls back to `/` for anything that leaves it or points at `/login` again, and `isAppPath` sends a target under `/api/` through a full page load, because the Scalar page is not a SvelteKit route. `isSignedOut` in `frontend/src/lib/api/client.ts` lets a form skip its own error toast for the `401` the client already turned into that redirect.

## Why it is this way

An own login form gives the two things an HTTP Basic prompt cannot: a logout, and a place to manage users from.

**The throttle and the argon2 cap.**

- The throttle keys on the username alone, because behind a reverse proxy the only address Rezepte sees is the proxy's, and reading `X-Forwarded-For` safely takes a list of trusted proxies an operator can get wrong without anything telling them.
- An attempt counts when it begins, not when its password turns out wrong: counted afterwards, a burst of parallel requests would all pass the check before the first failure landed.
- Unknown names are counted exactly like real ones, so a `429` never tells anyone which accounts exist; a full argon2 queue is reported for an unknown name exactly as for a real one, for the same reason, and `dummyHash` derives its one key outside the queue so that it can never fail.
- Counting unknown names is why the map is capped (`throttleCapacity`, 10,000 names) rather than limited to names that exist. When it is full, idle names go first, then the least recently seen name that still has free attempts, and a name that has used them only when every tracked name has: evicting a locked name hands it its free attempts back, and entries are made before the argon2 queue, so a flood of fresh names must not be able to reach one.
- The lock sits on the name, not on the password check, which keeps a locked attempt free: no query, no argon2.
- The concurrency cap covers what the lock cannot, many different names at once. It bounds memory, the resource an argon2 flood exhausts, and the queue bound keeps waiting bounded too: a caller turned away learns it at once, instead of every login, a household member's included, waiting behind whatever an attacker has queued.

Renaming somebody is not administration, which is why the profile write has a guard of its own instead of reusing `guardTarget`. `guardTarget` lets an admin act on a member, and that is the right rule for a role change or a password reset: those keep the instance usable, and any admin has to be able to perform them without waiting for the owner. Deciding what another adult in the household is called has nothing operational in it, so it stays with the account that owns the instance. Folding it into `guardTarget` would have handed it to every admin as a side effect of a rule written about something else, and the two could not then be separated again without taking a capability back off people who already had it.

Authorization at household scale stays a comparison of two fields, the actor's rank and the target's. `requireAdmin`, the self-delete refusal and the two pure functions in `permission.go` are the whole of it. The one configurable rule in the instance is the household lock on recipes, which [Editing rights](/features/editing-rights) owns.

The rank is what makes `admin` a role that is handed out and taken back. Without an account that outranks every other, any admin can delete, demote or reset the person who installed the instance, and a rule that only keeps the admin count from reaching zero protects nobody from that: it still leaves every admin able to remove every other. An account that can be neither deleted nor demoted always counts as an admin, and the admins below it are delegated rather than protected.

The contract document describes the surface of the running deployment, down to the admin endpoints — a fingerprint of that instance, which is more than a published spec gives away, and a self-hosted instance on the internet should give a stranger nothing. Deriving the protection from a prefix rather than from literal paths means a new flavor of the document is covered before anyone notices it exists.

The redirect-versus-`401` split is load-bearing rather than cosmetic. `docs/user/scripts/fetch-openapi.ts` decides by `res.ok`, so a redirect it followed to a `200` login page would look like success and write that page into `openapi.json`. Scalar's own fetch of the document asks for JSON and keeps the `401` as well.

Who takes part is not a secret inside a household, and hiding it would make the list of people a privilege: a member would see only the accounts that have written a recipe, and could not tell who may change their recipes or reset their password. `/people` is its own operation rather than a looser `/users` because the two answer different questions to different audiences - who is here, for everyone, and which account to manage, for admins - and one shape per operation keeps both contracts exact. Opening the list shows every login name, including accounts that have written nothing; the authors' names were already visible through `/authors` and `?author=`, and the login throttle keys on the name precisely so that knowing one buys nothing.

The browser picks the crop and the service cuts it. The dialog sends the original and three fractions, and package image is the only code that touches pixels, so the UI, an API client and an MCP tool reach one pixel path with one set of limits, one orientation rule and the same decode slots as a recipe photo. A client that cropped would have to be trusted to send a well-formed square, and a second client would have to implement the same thing.

## Rejected

- **Sortable columns on the people list.** Grouping by role already gives the one order worth choosing, and names are ordered inside each group; a household of a handful of accounts gains nothing from reversing it, and a sort head is a second control over the same job that a phone has no room for.
- **Opening `/api/v1/users` to every account and trimming the body by the caller's rank.** One operation with two shapes: `locale` and `createdAt` would become optional in the OpenAPI document for every client, admins' tokens included, and each caller would have to learn from the data which shape it got.
- **Growing `/api/v1/authors` into the people list.** It is a facet of the recipe list - who wrote how many - under the `recipes:read` scope; accounts without a recipe and roles have nothing to do with filtering recipes.
- **Limiting logins per client IP.** Behind the reverse proxy every documented setup uses, every request comes from the proxy, so a per-IP limit would lock the whole household out together unless Rezepte trusted `X-Forwarded-For` — and trusting it needs `REZEPTE_TRUSTED_PROXIES`, a setting that fails silently in both directions: too narrow and the limit is shared again, too wide and anyone can pick their own address.
- **Counting failures after the password check.** Parallel requests would all pass the check before the first failure was recorded.
- **HTTP Basic Auth.** No logout, and no user management.
- **A JWT in local storage.** Readable by any XSS, and with no server-side record there is nothing to revoke when a password changes.
- **A permission table or per-resource ACLs.** A schema and a lookup in place of two field comparisons, for three ranked roles and a handful of accounts.
- **A flag beside the role, such as `is_owner`.** Two columns holding one fact, and a `role` field that does not state the whole truth about an account: every reader has to fetch both and every writer has to keep them agreeing.
- **A separate `instance` table with a foreign key to the owner.** The fact leaves the user row, so every test that builds a user service has to seed a second table — for a guarantee the partial unique index and the two triggers already give.
- **A unique color per account.** A unique index on `color` caps the household at eight people and turns the ninth into a schema problem, and it makes picking a color a race rather than a preference - the swatch somebody was about to choose disappears under them. The picker marks a color another account already holds and lets it be chosen anyway, which is the whole of the constraint worth having.
- **A free color value rather than a palette token.** A hex string arrives as a background with no foreground to go with it, so the capital inside the circle has whatever contrast the person happened to land on, and one value cannot serve both themes. A closed set keeps the measured pairs in `frontend/src/app.css` and lets the API refuse everything else with an `enum` before a handler runs.
- **Soft-deleting users.** Little in the schema references a user beyond the recipes they wrote or edited, their comments and their sessions; reassigning the first, orphaning the second and cascading the rest beats a status column every query has to filter on.
- **Email as a login name or match key.** Nothing verifies a typed address, and a provider's address can be re-registered; `(iss, sub)` is the only link.
- **Restricting the contract routes further — to admins, or off entirely in production.** The contract is not admin-owned, and a logged-in member already knows the API from using the frontend; switching the docs page off takes it from the operator who has the most use for it instead of protecting it. A session of any role is the line.
- **zxcvbn's German and English language packs.** They add names, common words and Wikipedia vocabulary, and a megabyte gzipped on top of the 230 KB the leaked-password list costs; that list already catches most of what a household actually types.
- **Rating in the service.** No bundle cost, but a request per pause in typing and a Go port of zxcvbn that nobody maintains, against a standard-library-first service.
- **Refusing a weak password in the form.** A rule only the browser enforces is one API call away from not existing; a real refusal belongs in the service, next to the length rule.
- **Cropping on a canvas in the browser.** The upload would be a pre-cut bitmap: the service could not enforce a minimum, API and MCP clients would have to reimplement the crop, and a second pixel path would sit beside package image. The browser only reports the square it shows.
- **A two-step upload with a server-rendered preview.** A draft file to store and expire, and a second request before anything changes, for a preview the dialog already draws from the file in memory.
- **A crop library.** The math is a handful of pure functions in `frontend/src/lib/user/crop.ts` with their own tests, where a library would bring its own pointer handling and styling against the design system.
- **A throttle on setup-link redemption.** A 256-bit token cannot be guessed.
- **The token in the path.** It would land in proxy logs and Referer headers.

## Limits

- No account can use the admin reset on itself, and that falls out of the rank rule rather than being a case of its own: an admin's own row is an admin target, which only the owner may touch, and the owner's row is refused for every caller including the owner. Self-service is the profile page's own-password change, which verifies the current password and keeps the calling browser signed in - unless the account has none yet, in which case there is nothing to verify and the call sets one outright. The reset still ends every session of the account it is used on.
- The first start needs `REZEPTE_ADMIN_PASSWORD` while the `users` table is empty — `EnsureSuperadmin` returns `ErrAdminPasswordRequired` without it, and the service does not come up. `REZEPTE_ADMIN_USER` names that account and defaults to `admin`. Once an owner exists `EnsureSuperadmin` is a no-op and neither variable creates anything; a `users` table that holds rows but no owner is `ErrNoSuperadmin`, and the service refuses to start. Both values are taken after the substitution `--demo` performs (see [Demo mode](/features/demo-mode)), so a demo run without `REZEPTE_ADMIN_PASSWORD` supplies both credentials itself: nothing is required of the environment, and a `REZEPTE_ADMIN_USER` set there is discarded before anything reads it.
- Whoever knows a username can keep that account locked by failing on purpose, for at most 15 minutes after their last attempt — accepted, because the alternative keys need client addresses Rezepte cannot see behind a proxy. The owner is not exempt. Sessions already signed in are untouched, and `rezepte --reset-superadmin-password` does not go through the throttle.
- Because an attempt counts when it begins, several correct logins for one name started at the same moment count against each other, and past the fifth they are refused with `429`. People do not sign in that way; a script sharing one account across parallel workers would have to.
- The own-password change is not throttled. It takes a session of that account already, so it is no way in for someone who has none, and its argon2 evaluations sit in the same queue as every other.
- The throttle lives in memory: a restart forgets every lock, and it counts per process, which is all a single-binary SQLite deployment runs. A locked name is evicted only once 10,000 names are tracked and every one of them has used its free attempts — about 50,000 argon2 evaluations, four at a time.
- A flood of logins that keeps the argon2 queue full turns everybody else away with `503` for as long as it lasts: the cap trades availability for bounded memory, and a household member retries.
- There is no handover, and the owner has no reset from inside the application, not even by mail. The bootstrap account is the owner for the life of the instance, no endpoint promotes a second one, and a forgotten owner password locks the instance out of its own administration until someone with shell access to the host runs `rezepte --reset-superadmin-password`. On a self-hosted household instance, giving up that account means giving up the instance.
