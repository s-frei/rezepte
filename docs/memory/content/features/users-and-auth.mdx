Several people in a household share one recipe collection. Accounts are created, re-roled, reset and removed in the UI; only the instance owner comes from the environment. Every account carries a display name and a palette color on top of its login name, which together are how the UI names and marks a person; [Design system](/architecture/design-system) owns the tokens the color resolves to. [HTTP API](/architecture/api) owns the endpoints, their bodies and their status codes; [Data model](/architecture/data-model) owns the `users` and `sessions` tables.

## How it works

**A login form, and argon2id underneath it.** `frontend/src/routes/login/+page.svelte` posts username and password to the login operation; `user.Service.Authenticate` trims the username the way `Create` trims it and runs `VerifyPassword`. `HashPassword` in `service/internal/user/password.go` produces a PHC string (`$argon2id$…`) with 64 MiB of memory, one iteration, four lanes, a 16-byte salt and a 32-byte key. `VerifyPassword` reads those parameters back out of the stored hash and bounds each of them — at most 1 GiB, 16 iterations, 32 lanes — so a tampered row cannot make one login attempt allocate a gigabyte. An unknown username is still charged one argon2 evaluation, against a `dummyHash` computed once per process, so timing does not separate "no such user" from "wrong password".

**A name that keeps failing is locked, and argon2 runs four at a time.** `auth.Service.Login` asks the throttle in `service/internal/auth/throttle.go` before it touches the password. The throttle counts attempts per username, folded the way the `users` table compares it: trimmed like `Authenticate` trims it, with ASCII letters lowercased and every other letter left alone, which is what the column's `COLLATE NOCASE` does. Five attempts are free, the fifth arms a 30-second lock, and every attempt after a lock has run out doubles it up to 15 minutes. A locked name gets `*auth.ThrottledError` straight away, which the handler turns into `429` with `Retry-After` in whole seconds, rounded up; the SPA shows `login_throttled`. A successful login forgets the name, and so does a day without attempts, swept on the same tick as expired sessions. Beneath it, `deriveKey` in `service/internal/user/password.go` runs at most `maxConcurrentArgon` (4) argon2 derivations at once, for logins, password changes and new hashes alike, and lets at most `maxQueuedArgon` (32) more wait for a slot. A waiter whose request context ends gives up its place, and a caller that finds the queue full gets `user.ErrBusy`, which `auth.BusyError` turns into `503` with `Retry-After: 1` on every operation that hashes or checks a password. `auth.DeclareRetryAfter` puts that header into the OpenAPI document too, because huma writes error responses from an operation's `Errors` list without headers.

**The token lives in the cookie, its digest in the table.** `Login` in `service/internal/auth/service.go` draws 32 random bytes, encodes them with `base64.RawURLEncoding` and stores only the SHA-256 of that string as the session id, so a database leak yields no usable session. The token goes back as the `rezepte_session` cookie: `HttpOnly`, `Path=/`, `SameSite=Lax`, and `Secure` when `REZEPTE_SECURE_COOKIES` is set. `SessionTTL` is 30 days and slides — `Authenticate` resets it to a full 30 days once less than `SessionTTL - renewAfter` is left, with `renewAfter` at 24 hours, so an active session is written back at most once a day. The renewal is reported back as `Verified.Renewed`, and both callers re-issue the cookie with the fresh expiry so the browser's copy never falls behind the row. A session found past its expiry is deleted on the spot; `DeleteExpired` also runs once at startup and `SweepLoop` every 24 hours after that.

**One middleware, two authenticators.** `auth.Middleware` authenticates every operation that declares `Security` — `auth.SessionSecurity` for session-only operations, `auth.Protected(scopes...)` for the ones an API token may also reach; an operation without `Security` is public. It is installed through `httpserver.WithAPIMiddleware`, and `httpserver.New` applies every `Option` before it returns — so there is no window in which an operation could be registered ahead of it. A request carrying an `Authorization: Bearer` header is decided by a branch inside `Middleware` alone, with no fallback to the cookie; [API tokens](/features/api-tokens) owns its outcomes. `auth.TokenOnly(scopes...)` is the third kind of `Security`, for an operation no session may reach. The only routes that are not operations and still need authentication are the contract routes; they take `auth.RequireAuthOrLogin` from `service/internal/auth/require.go`, plain `func(http.Handler) http.Handler` middleware with the same session authentication and the same renewal re-issue.

**CSRF is `SameSite=Lax` plus an origin check.** `checkOrigin` in `service/internal/httpserver/origin.go` refuses a `POST`, `PUT`, `PATCH` or `DELETE` under `/api/`, and a request of any method to `/mcp`, whose `Origin` header names a host other than the request's (or cannot be parsed). `/mcp` is covered whatever the method because the MCP Streamable HTTP transport requires every server to validate `Origin` and answer a present, invalid one with `403`. A request that carries no `Origin` passes: that is a non-browser client, which the cookie's `SameSite` does not constrain either. [HTTP API](/architecture/api) has the response body.

**Three ranked roles, compared in one file.** `user.RoleUser`, `user.RoleAdmin` and `user.RoleSuperadmin` are the whole set, in ascending rank. The superadmin is the instance owner and is an admin, so `Role.IsAdmin` and `Role.IsSuperadmin` in `service/internal/user/service.go` are the only way to ask what a role carries: a bare comparison against `RoleAdmin` excludes the owner and is always a bug. `requireAdmin` in `service/internal/userapi/handler.go` pulls the authenticated user out of the request context, answers `401` when there is none and `403` `admin role required` when `IsAdmin` is false; all four user management operations call it first, and the two that name a target — update and delete — pass the caller it returns into the service. The SPA has the same trap and the same single answer: `isAdminRole` in `frontend/src/lib/roles.ts` is where it asks, because `role === 'admin'` locks the owner out of every admin surface it guards, `frontend/src/lib/settings/row-permissions.ts` and the admin-only parts of the people page among them. `roleLabel` sits in that same module and is the only place a role becomes the label text a person reads, so the profile card and the people list cannot disagree about what to call one. `roleRank` sits there too, for the order the people list gives the three roles. Everything finer than "is this an admin" lives in `service/internal/user/permission.go` and nowhere else: `guardTarget` says who may act on whom and, applied to a role instead of a row, who may hand out which role; `CanAssignRole` is that second use, exported for the create path, which has a requested role but no target row to read. One sentence is the whole rule: an admin manages plain users; anything touching an admin or the superadmin belongs to the superadmin; and the superadmin can be neither deleted nor demoted by anyone, themselves included. There is no permission table: for accounts, the actor's rank against the target's is the entire authorization model. Recipes carry the one per-resource rule, which [Editing rights](/features/editing-rights) owns.

**Every account carries a display name and a color.** `display_name` is what the UI prints wherever it names somebody and `color` is the palette token that marks them at a glance; neither is an identity, and neither is unique - `username` stays the only thing that identifies an account. `user.Create` is the single writer of a user row, which is why it owns both defaults: an empty display name becomes the trimmed username, and an empty color becomes the least-used color of the palette with ties broken by palette order (`leastUsed` in `service/internal/user/profile.go`, over the counts `ColorUsage` groups out of the table). Least-used rather than first-free because duplicates are allowed, so the two only part company once every color is held, and spreading a household across the palette is the property worth keeping. `normalizeDisplayName` trims, falls back to the username when nothing printable is left, refuses more than 64 **runes** and refuses a control character - so the column is `NOT NULL`, is never empty, and no client has to invent a fallback of its own. `ParseColor` accepts the palette exactly as written, with no trimming and no case folding: the value comes from a closed set the API declares as an `enum`, so anything else is a client error rather than a typo to repair. Self-service is `PATCH /api/v1/auth/me/profile`, which every role may use on its own row, and `GET /api/v1/auth/me/colors` gives the picker its count per color - counts only, no names, because a mark on a swatch is all the picker needs. Writing somebody **else's** profile is the owner's alone: `user.CanEditProfile` in `service/internal/user/permission.go`, checked in `update-user` before anything is written, so a body that mixes a rename with a password reset changes neither.

**Every account may carry a picture.** `users.avatar_id` names it, `NULL` means none, and a circle without one is the color and initial. The account sets its own through `PUT /api/v1/auth/me/avatar` and removes it through `DELETE` on the same path; the owner does both for any account through `/api/v1/users/{id}/avatar`, the same rule `user.CanEditProfile` applies to the rest of a profile, so an admin or a member cannot touch somebody else's picture. `service/internal/avatar` holds it: one square JPEG per account at `<REZEPTE_DATA_DIR>/avatars/<user id>/<avatar id>.jpg`, rendered by package image ([Images](/features/images) owns the pixels), with a fresh `avatar_id` for every upload, so a changed picture is a changed URL and the file is cached for a year. `Service.Set` renders the file first, swaps the row in a transaction, and removes the previous file only once the row names the new one; a failed swap removes the new file, so the row never names a missing file and a failure leaves the old picture in place. `Remove` clears the column and deletes the file, and removing a picture that is not there is not an error. `delete-user` calls `RemoveAll` after the row is gone, which removes the account's whole directory. Every session, and a token with `users:read`, may read the files; [HTTP API](/architecture/api) owns the routes and [Design system](/architecture/design-system) the circle that shows them.

**An account may also carry an email address, and it is profile data, not an identity.** `email` sits beside `display_name` and `color`: not unique, no part of signing in, and settable through the same `PATCH /api/v1/auth/me/profile` the name and color go through, with `ParseEmail` in `service/internal/user/email.go` accepting only a bare address (no display name, no angle brackets) and treating "" as "none". `email_verified` is true only when an identity provider ([OIDC](/features/oidc)) vouched for the address through its own `email_verified` claim; typing or changing the address by hand always clears the mark, even when the new spelling names the same mailbox the provider vouched for - `SetProfile` compares the raw column, not the parsed address. `(*user.Service).SetEmailIfEmpty` is the other writer: it fills the column only while it is still empty, so an identity provider's address becomes the account's own the first time somebody signs in through it, and is left alone from then on - a person's typed address always outranks whatever a provider offers afterwards. Neither field appears on `/people` or `/users`; `userapi.UserAccount` and `PersonEntry` stay exactly as before.

**An account also carries whether it may share publicly.** `users.can_share_publicly` is an admin's per-person switch, on by default, in the `UserAccount` the API returns as `canSharePublicly`; `PATCH /users/{id}` accepts it under `guardTarget` like a reset: an admin switches it for plain members, only the owner for admins, and the owner's own row always answers `409` `the superadmin cannot be modified`, since [public sharing](/features/public-shares) for the whole instance is the owner's household-wide switch, not a per-person right on their own account. One rank rule for the role change, the reset and this switch is what lets `update-user` take them in one body without one landing while another is refused; an admin aiming at their own row is aiming at an admin, so nobody re-grants themselves a right another admin withdrew. The people list shows the toggle exactly where it shows reset and delete. Withdrawing it pauses the person's existing links rather than deleting them; [Public shares](/features/public-shares) owns the validity rule.

**The matrix, once, so it need not be re-derived.** Rows are the caller, columns the target; a cell says what the API answers. Creation has no target row, so it is `CanAssignRole` alone: an admin may ask for `user`, only the owner may ask for `admin`, and `superadmin` is refused by huma's `enum:"admin,user"` with `422` before a handler runs.

| Caller | Target: a member | Target: an admin | Target: the owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| member | `403` `admin role required`, from `requireAdmin` before rank is consulted | same | same |
| admin | delete, reset and the public-sharing switch; a role change and a profile write are each `403` `superadmin role required` | `403` `superadmin role required` | `409` `the superadmin cannot be modified` |
| the owner | delete, reset, re-role, rename, recolor and the public-sharing switch | delete, reset, re-role, rename, recolor and the public-sharing switch | `409` `the superadmin cannot be modified` for delete, reset, re-role and the public-sharing switch; a profile write lands |

The profile write is the one column that never reads the target's rank: `guardProfileEdit` asks only whether the caller is the owner, so the owner reaches every row including their own, and everybody else is `403` `superadmin role required` whoever they aim at. The people list hides the action on the caller's own row all the same - the profile page is the one place a person renames themselves, so nobody has to wonder which of two forms is the real one. Two further rules cut across every cell: nobody deletes their own account (`409` `cannot delete yourself`, checked before the transaction opens), and nobody resets their own password through the admin path — an admin aiming at their own row is aiming at an admin. `docs/user/content/guide/users.mdx` states the same matrix as what a person sees in the row, since an action the caller may not take is never rendered.

**The people list decides its own order.** `ListUsers` is a plain `ORDER BY username`, and the column's `COLLATE NOCASE` decides what that means: case-insensitive across ASCII and nothing else, so it files "Änna" behind "Zoe". That order is the API's contract for its own consumers and stays as it is. What the page shows is the client's answer instead: `groupPeople` in `frontend/src/lib/settings/people-groups.ts` makes one group per role that somebody holds, in `roleRank` order - owner, admins, members - and orders each group by display name with an `Intl.Collator` over Paraglide's `getLocale()`, so a second locale sorts by its own rules. The order must stay total, which is why a collator tie falls through to a code-point comparison, then to the username (`compareUsernames`, which does the same) and then to the id: `numeric: true` calls "user2" and "user02" equal, a stable sort then keeps whatever order the rows arrived in, and that is the moving row all over again. `PeopleList.svelte` derives the groups from the list it is handed, which is why a freshly created account lands where it will sit after a reload, and a promoted one moves under "Admins" the moment the change is saved.

**Every account sees who takes part.** `GET /api/v1/people` (`list-people`, in `userapi` beside the user operations) answers any session with every account as a `PersonEntry` - `id`, `username`, `displayName`, `color`, `avatarId`, `role` - and nothing else: no language, no creation date. It is the one operation in the package that does not call `requireAdmin`; it asks `auth.UserFrom` for a caller and nothing more. Its key set is pinned field by field in `TestPeopleHidesAccountDetails`, so a field added to the struct has to be added to the test on purpose, and `TestPeopleNeedsTheUsersReadScope` holds tokens to `users:read`. The frontend type carries the same name, `PersonEntry` in `frontend/src/lib/api/users.ts`, because `Person` in `frontend/src/lib/api/recipes.ts` is the roleless person a recipe names, and two exports called `Person` would be one wrong import away from each other. The page under Settings → People (`frontend/src/routes/settings/users/+page.svelte`; the path says `users` because it is also where accounts are managed) reads `/people` for every viewer, admins included; what differs by rank is what surrounds the list. A member gets a line saying admins manage accounts, and no controls and no owner hint - words about actions they do not have; on a phone only their own row opens anything, and that is their profile page; an admin gets "Add account", the color counts the dialogs need, the Recipe editing card, and per row whatever `rowPermissions` in `frontend/src/lib/settings/row-permissions.ts` grants. That function asks the viewer's rank first, because the page is open to every account, then mirrors `permission.go` for the rest; `PersonRow.svelte` and, on a phone, `PersonSheet.svelte` only render what it returns. A role change re-creates the row in its new group, so focus is put back by id rather than by element (`frontend/src/lib/settings/person-focus.ts`): the select in the moved row takes it on a wide screen, and on a phone the sheet stays open and hands focus to the row's button when it closes, or before it hands over to the reset, delete or profile dialog, which return focus there in turn. Deleting removes that row, so once it is gone the card's heading (`tabindex="-1"`) takes focus instead of `<body>`. A role takes effect with one tap, on either screen, so the toast that confirms it names the new role and carries "Undo", which sends the previous role back through the same path.

**Three words, three things.** *User* and *account* name the login and its management: `/api/v1/users`, the `users:*` scopes, "Add account". *Person* and *people* name who takes part in the household: `/api/v1/people`, the `Person` a recipe's `createdBy` carries, the "People" tab. *Member* names one role, `user`, and nothing else. The German catalog draws the same line ("Benutzer" for the account, "Mitglied" for the role), so no label in either language can be read as both.

**A token carries its owner's rank, never more.** `auth.TokenService.Authenticate` re-reads the owner's row on every request, so a token never outlives or exceeds the role its owner currently holds, and the scopes `auth.Protected` requires then narrow what the token may do within that role. Rank sits underneath both: `permission.go` sees the account a credential belongs to, so a `users:write` token issued by an admin who is not the owner cannot hand out the `admin` role any more than that admin can. See [API tokens](/features/api-tokens).

**User management and token management are their own packages because of the import direction.** `auth` imports `user`, and both the user handlers and the token handlers need `auth.UserFrom` and `auth.SessionSecurity` or `auth.Protected`. Registering either from `user`, or from `auth` itself, would close the cycle, so they live in `userapi` and `tokenapi` instead.

**A member may change their own password.** That and the own profile are the two user writes a non-admin can do, and both sit next to `me` rather than on the admin resource. `user.Service.ChangePassword` verifies the current password before storing the new one; a mismatch is `user.ErrWrongPassword`, which the handler turns into a `422` located at `body.currentPassword`.

**Setup links instead of handed-over passwords.** Creating a user with no `password` (`POST /api/v1/users`, `createInput.Body.Password` is `omitempty`) stores an empty `password_hash` and, in the same response, issues a setup link: `auth.Service.IssueSetupLink` draws 32 random bytes the way a session token is drawn, stores only `hashToken` of it in `setup_links` (`0010_setup_links.sql`), and returns the raw token once. `setup_links` holds one row per account - `setup_links_user_idx` is a unique index on `user_id` - so `ReplaceSetupLink`'s `ON CONFLICT (user_id) DO UPDATE` is the whole of "issuing another replaces it": there is never a second open link to reason about, and the old token stops resolving the moment a new one is stored. `auth.SetupPath` puts the token in the URL fragment, `"/welcome#" + token`, never a query string or path segment - a fragment never leaves the browser, so it reaches neither a server log nor a `Referer` header. `auth.SetupLinkTTL` is 7 days, swept by `DeleteExpired` alongside expired sessions. Issuing a link is the same act as a password reset and follows the same rank rule: `user.CanManage` is `guardTarget` under a name that reads right for "may reset or hand this account a fresh credential," called from `IssueSetupLink` and from `RevokeSetupLink`, which deletes the row if one exists and is not an error when none does - a revoke and a fresh issue are the same button in the UI, and revoking a link nobody opened yet is not a mistake to report. `PATCH /api/v1/users/{id}`'s password reset follows the same rank rule. Every setup operation is session-only (`auth.SessionSecurity`), never `auth.Protected` - issuing a credential is not a thing a `users:write` token should be able to do unattended, the same boundary [API tokens](/features/api-tokens) draws around the admin reset. An API token creating a user cannot leave `password` empty either, for a different reason: no setup link can ever reach it, since redeeming one takes the admin UI's dialog, which a token has no way to show. `create-user` refuses an empty password from a bearer caller with `422` at `body.password` ("a password is required when creating an account with an API token"), deciding "bearer caller" with `auth.ViaToken(ctx)` - a context flag `auth.Middleware`'s bearer branch sets and a session never does. `GET /api/v1/users` calls `sessions.OpenSetupLinks` once per request and folds `setupLinkExpiresAt` into each row that has one open, so the people list can show "open until" without a request per row; `hasPassword` rides along on every row unconditionally, and `auth.UserResponse` carries it too. Redemption is the public half: `POST /api/v1/auth/setup/inspect` (`PeekSetupLink`) answers a token with the username and display name to greet, without spending it, so `/welcome` can render before anyone has typed a password; `POST /api/v1/auth/setup/password` (`RedeemWithPassword`) hashes the new password **before** touching the link - argon2 first, so a full queue (`user.ErrBusy` → `503`) leaves the link open for a retry instead of burning it on a request that never wrote anything - then opens a transaction that does the delete-returning consume (`consumeWith`, the one place both `ConsumeSetupLink` and `RedeemWithPassword` map a spent or unknown token to `ErrNoSetupLink`), the password write and `DeleteUserSessionsExcept` (empty keep-token: every prior session of the account ends, an invite is not assumed to be the same person's only browser) as one unit; a second, concurrent redemption of the same token finds no row left to delete and gets `ErrNoSetupLink`, so of two requests racing on one link exactly one ever sets a password - `TestRedeemSetupLinkOnlyOnce` runs the race under `-race`. Unknown, expired and already-used all answer the same `ErrNoSetupLink` → `404`, one message for all three so a probe learns nothing about which case it hit. A successful redemption signs the account in on the spot, through `StartSession` - the same call `Login` uses after its own password check - so accepting an invite and being signed in are one step.

**The setup link in the UI.** "Add account" (`CreateUserDialog.svelte`) offers a choice above the password fields: "Send a setup link", selected by default, and "Set a password now"; the password fields and their strength meter render, and validate, only in the second mode. A link-mode create closes the dialog and opens `SetupLinkDialog.svelte` with the token, which shows the full URL in a wrapped, read-only, selectable `<textarea>` next to a copy button (a failed or unavailable `navigator.clipboard` is caught and answered with a toast pointing at the selectable text instead) and the expiry date. The same dialog opens from a row's "Setup link" action, next to "Reset password" under the same `manageAccount` rank rule, at any later point - which replaces whatever link was open. Under a person's name, for an admin who may manage that account: an open link reads "Setup link open until" the expiry date, with a "Revoke link" action beside it, and an account with neither a password, a connected identity provider ([OIDC](/features/oidc)) nor an open link reads "Not set up yet"; both the row action and the revoke action carry the person's name in their accessible name, since a flat list of rows otherwise repeats the same button label once per person. `frontend/src/routes/welcome/+page.svelte` is where a link lands: it reads the token from `location.hash` once, on mount, and immediately replaces it out of the address bar with `history.replaceState` - whether or not the form is ever finished - so an abandoned invite never sits as a live token in browser history. It calls `inspectSetupLink` to greet the person by name and `redeemSetupLink` on submit to set their password and sign them in. A 404 from either call (the link was never valid, or was used or expired since the page loaded) is the only case that shows "This link no longer works"; any other inspect failure shows a generic error with a "Try again" retry, since the token stays in the page's own memory regardless; a 422 on submit maps to the password field through the same `passwordErrorsFromApi` every other password form uses. The tab title only ever shows the person's name once an invite has actually loaded, never a bare "Welcome, ". When the browser already holds a session for another account (the root layout's `me()` found one; `/welcome` is exempt from its login redirect), a notice above the form says who is signed in and whom the link is for; nothing else changes, and finishing the link replaces that session.

**An account may have no password.** `password_hash` is `''`, not `NULL` - a bare `TEXT NOT NULL` needs no migration to add the state, and every reader already treats the column as a string. `user.Create` stores that empty hash whenever `CreateParams.Password` is empty, which is the shape an account set up by something other than a typed password has; the one account this is never true for is the superadmin, since `Create` refuses `RoleSuperadmin` with an empty password with `ErrAdminPasswordRequired` - the instance's own way back in always has one. `Authenticate` treats an empty stored hash exactly like a wrong password: it still runs one `VerifyPassword` against `dummyHash`, so neither the timing nor the response tells a caller a name exists with no password to guess. `ChangePassword` skips the current-password check the same way the profile write skips it: the caller's own session is already proof enough, so `PATCH /api/v1/auth/me` accepts a body with no `currentPassword` and the request only differs from a password reset by taking a session instead of an admin actor. Once a password is set the account is ordinary again - the next `ChangePassword` demands the current one like any other row.

**Every password field is written for a password manager.** A manager fills and saves by `autocomplete` alone, so the login form marks its fields `username` and `current-password`, and every field that sets a password is `new-password`, which is also what makes a browser offer a generated one. The login's username field turns off `autocapitalize` and `spellcheck` as well, because a phone keyboard that capitalizes `sam` to `Sam` fails the login. `PasswordForm.svelte` has no visible username, so it carries a hidden, read-only `autocomplete="username"` field holding the caller's own: without it a manager cannot tell which account the changed password belongs to and saves it as a second entry. The two admin dialogs stay without one on purpose, because the password they set belongs to somebody else. The login form's username field carries `autofocus`, and it has to be the attribute rather than a `focus()` call on mount: SvelteKit's `reset_focus` moves focus to `<body>` after every navigation, the redirect to `/login` included, and spares only an element marked `autofocus`.

**A new password is rated while it is typed, and only rated.** `PasswordStrength.svelte` sits under the field in all three forms that set a password - the own change, "Add account" and the admin reset - and shows zxcvbn's score as four segments and a label. `estimateStrength` in `frontend/src/lib/settings/password-strength.ts` owns the estimate: `@zxcvbn-ts/core` with `@zxcvbn-ts/language-common` (the leaked-password list and the keyboard graphs), both behind a dynamic `import()`, so the roughly 230 KB gzipped reach a browser the first time somebody types into one of those fields and never on the login or a recipe page. It passes the account's username and display name, whole and word by word, plus `rezepte` and `recipes`, so `anna1990` is weak for Anna and nobody else. The component waits 250 ms after the last keystroke and drops a result the field has already moved past; the label is a polite live region, so a screen reader hears the rating once per pause. Nothing is blocked: the length rule the API enforces is the only rule a password must pass.

**Every form that sets a password takes it twice and states the minimum.** The own change, "Add account" and the admin reset check the pair with `validateNewPassword` in `frontend/src/lib/settings/password.ts` before anything is sent, and a mismatch is the repeat field's error; the API never sees the second copy. The new-password field carries "At least 8 characters" as its hint - the same message the too-short error uses, so a failed save turns the grey hint red in place instead of adding a second line.

**Every password change revokes sessions, and the two kinds differ only in what they keep.** Both go through `auth.Service.DeleteUserSessionsExcept`. The own change passes the calling cookie's token, so the browser making the change stays signed in and every other device is signed out. The admin reset passes an empty token, which keeps nothing: a reset means the old credential is forgotten or compromised.

**The rank guard runs inside the write transaction.** `user.Service.Delete` refuses `actor.ID == id` with `ErrSelfDelete`, a plain comparison before any transaction opens. Everything else is `guardTarget`, which `SetRole`, `Delete` and `SetPassword` run on the row they have just read, inside the write transaction `db.Tx` opens — it takes its write lock at `BEGIN` because of the connection DSN [Data model](/architecture/data-model) lists, so a concurrent promotion cannot slip the target out from under a check that has already passed. `SetRole` runs `guardTarget` a second time, on the role it is about to write. `SetPassword` hashes before opening the transaction, because holding the `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` lock for an argon2id run would block every other writer for the length of it. Each refusal carries one status code: `ErrSuperadminProtected` is `409` `the superadmin cannot be modified`, a fact about the instance that holds for every caller and the register `ErrSelfDelete` already uses, and `ErrSuperadminRequired` is `403` `superadmin role required`, which is about who is asking. Two SQLite triggers, `users_superadmin_undeletable` and `users_superadmin_role_locked`, refuse the delete and the demotion underneath the Go layer, for a raw query or a code path that forgets to ask; [Data model](/architecture/data-model) owns them. Deleting a user also reassigns the recipes they created **or last edited** to the acting admin - the token's owner when the call carried a bearer token, since `requireAdmin` reads whichever account the credential belongs to - through `ReassignRecipes` in the same transaction — `created_by` and `updated_by` both reference `users` and neither has an `ON DELETE` clause, so a recipe somebody else wrote but this user last touched would hold the delete on its foreign key. `ReassignRecipes` leaves `edit_policy` alone, so each recipe keeps its [editing rights](/features/editing-rights) under its new author. Their sessions go with the row (see [Data model](/architecture/data-model)).

**The bootstrap account is the owner, and only a shell resets it.** `EnsureSuperadmin` runs as the process starts and has three answers: an empty instance gets the account `REZEPTE_ADMIN_USER` names, created with `RoleSuperadmin` through `Create`, so it picks up the same profile defaults as every other account - its display name is its login name, and its color is `amber`, which is what the least-used rule yields on an empty table: every count is zero and the tie goes to palette order; an instance that already has an owner is left alone; and a populated `users` table with no owner is `ErrNoSuperadmin`, which stops the service from coming up rather than let it serve an instance nobody owns. `superadmin` is never an assignable role — `POST /users` and `PATCH /users/{id}` declare `enum:"admin,user"`, so huma answers `422` before a handler runs — and the owner's password is theirs alone: the admin reset refuses that target for every caller, the owner included, and self-service goes through `ChangePassword` on the profile page. That leaves one way back from a forgotten owner password, and it is outside the application. `rezepte --reset-superadmin-password` takes the new password from `REZEPTE_ADMIN_PASSWORD`, writes it to the owner, ends every session of that account and returns without starting the server; reaching it needs shell access to the host, which is the operator by definition.

**The contract routes require authentication too, a session or a token.** `isSpecRoute` in `service/internal/httpserver/api.go` matches `/api/v1/docs`, everything under the `/api/v1/openapi` prefix and everything under `/api/v1/schemas/`. huma hangs those off the mux itself rather than registering them as operations, so `auth.Middleware` never sees them; `httpserver.WithSpecGuard` wraps them in front of the mux instead, and `service/cmd/rezepte/main.go` supplies `auth.RequireAuthOrLogin`. Any role is enough — reading the contract is not an administrative act — and a valid API token opens it too, with no scope required: `RequireAuthOrLogin` checks none, so a valid token is sufficient on its own. A `Server` built without the option serves them openly, so the package itself stays unopinionated. Matching by prefix rather than by an explicit list costs one rule: no operation may ever be registered at a path under those prefixes.

**A browser is redirected, everything else gets a `401`.** A bearer request is never redirected: `RequireAuthOrLogin` answers a bad token with a plain `401`, because a redirect to the login page is meaningless to a client that authenticates with a header rather than a cookie. For a session, `navigatingBrowser` accepts a `GET` whose `Accept` contains `text/html` — someone typing the address — and that gets a `303` to `/login?next=<escaped request URI>`; a bare `*/*`, which is what `fetch` and curl send, does not count, and neither does any other method, so those keep the `401` problem body. The SPA closes the loop: `safeNext` in `frontend/src/lib/api/auth.ts` resolves `next` against a fixed fake origin and falls back to `/` for anything that leaves that origin or points at `/login` again, and `isAppPath` routes a target under `/api/` through a full page load, because `goto` is client-side routing and the Scalar page is not a SvelteKit route. `isSignedOut` in `frontend/src/lib/api/client.ts` lets a form skip its own error toast for the `401` the client already turned into that redirect.

## Why it is this way

An own login form gives the two things an HTTP Basic prompt cannot: a logout, and a place to manage users from.

The throttle keys on the username alone, because behind a reverse proxy the only address Rezepte sees is the proxy's, and reading `X-Forwarded-For` safely takes a list of trusted proxies that an operator can get wrong without anything telling them. An attempt counts when it begins, not when its password turns out wrong: counted afterwards, a burst of parallel requests would all pass the check before the first failure landed. Unknown names are counted exactly like real ones, so a `429` never tells anyone which accounts exist; a full argon2 queue is reported for an unknown name exactly as for a real one, for the same reason, and `dummyHash` derives its one key outside the queue so that it can never fail. Counting unknown names is also why the map is capped (`throttleCapacity`, 10,000 names) rather than limited to names that exist. When it is full, idle names go first, then the least recently seen name that still has free attempts, and a name that has used them only when every tracked name has: evicting a locked name hands it its free attempts back, and entries cost nothing to create — they are made before the argon2 queue — so a flood of fresh names must not be able to reach one. The lock on the name, not on the password check, is what keeps a locked attempt free: no query, no argon2. The concurrency cap covers what the lock cannot, many different names at once. It bounds memory, the resource an argon2 flood actually exhausts, and the queue bound keeps waiting time bounded too: a caller turned away learns it at once, instead of every login, a household member's included, waiting behind whatever an attacker has queued.

Renaming somebody is not administration, which is why the profile write has a guard of its own instead of reusing `guardTarget`. `guardTarget` lets an admin act on a member, and that is the right rule for a role change or a password reset: those keep the instance usable, and any admin has to be able to perform them without waiting for the owner. Deciding what another adult in the household is called has nothing operational in it, so it stays with the account that owns the instance. Folding it into `guardTarget` would have handed it to every admin as a side effect of a rule written about something else, and the two would then have been impossible to separate again without taking a capability back off people who already had it.

Authorization at household scale stays a field comparison — of two fields, the actor's rank and the target's. `requireAdmin`, the self-delete refusal and the two pure functions in `permission.go` are the whole of it. The one configurable rule in the instance is the household lock on recipes, and [Editing rights](/features/editing-rights) owns it.

The rank is what makes `admin` a role that is handed out and taken back. Without an account that outranks every other, any admin can delete, demote or reset the person who installed the instance, and a rule that only keeps the admin count from reaching zero protects nobody from that: it still leaves every admin able to remove every other. An account that can be neither deleted nor demoted always counts as an admin, and the admins below it are delegated rather than protected.

The contract document describes the surface of the running deployment, down to the admin endpoints — a fingerprint of that instance, which is more than a published spec gives away, and a self-hosted instance on the internet should give a stranger nothing. Deriving the protection from a prefix rather than from six literal paths means an upgrade that adds a fifth flavor of the document is covered before anyone notices it exists.

The redirect-versus-`401` split is load-bearing rather than cosmetic. `docs/user/scripts/fetch-openapi.ts` decides by `res.ok`, so a redirect it followed to a `200` login page would look like success and write that page into `openapi.json`. Scalar's own fetch of the document asks for JSON and keeps the `401` as well.

Who takes part is not a secret inside a household, and hiding it would make the list of people a privilege: a member would see only the accounts that have written a recipe, and could not tell who may change their recipes or reset their password. `/people` is its own operation rather than a looser `/users` because the two answer different questions to different audiences - who is here, for everyone, and which account to manage, for admins - and one shape per operation keeps both contracts exact. Opening the list shows every login name, including accounts that have written nothing; the authors' names were already visible through `/authors` and `?author=`, and the login throttle keys on the name precisely so that knowing one buys nothing.

The browser picks the crop and the service cuts it. The dialog sends the original and three fractions, and package image is the only code that touches pixels, so the UI, an API client and an MCP tool reach one pixel path with one set of limits, one orientation rule and the same decode slots as a recipe photo. A client that cropped would have to be trusted to send a well-formed square, and a second client would have to implement the same thing.

## Rejected

- **Sortable columns on the people list.** Grouping by role already gives the one order worth choosing, and names are ordered inside each group; a household of a handful of accounts gains nothing from reversing it, and a sort head is a second control over the same job that a phone has no room for.
- **Opening `/api/v1/users` to every account and trimming the body by the caller's rank.** One operation with two shapes: `locale` and `createdAt` would become optional in the OpenAPI document for every client, admins' tokens included, and each caller would have to learn from the data which shape it got.
- **Growing `/api/v1/authors` into the people list.** It is a facet of the recipe list - who wrote how many - under the `recipes:read` scope; accounts without a recipe and roles have nothing to do with filtering recipes.
- **Limiting logins per client IP.** Behind the reverse proxy every documented setup uses, every request comes from the proxy, so a per-IP limit would lock the whole household out together unless Rezepte trusted `X-Forwarded-For` — and trusting it needs `REZEPTE_TRUSTED_PROXIES`, a setting that fails silently in both directions: too narrow and the limit is shared again, too wide and anyone can pick their own address.
- **Counting failures after the password check.** Parallel requests would all pass the check before the first failure was recorded.
- **HTTP Basic Auth.** No logout, and no user management.
- **A JWT in local storage.** Readable by any XSS, and with no server-side record there is nothing to revoke when a password changes.
- **A permission table or per-resource ACLs.** A schema and a lookup in place of two field comparisons, for three ranked roles and a handful of accounts.
- **A flag beside the role, such as `is_owner`.** Two columns holding one fact, and a `role` field that does not state the whole truth about an account: every reader has to fetch both and every writer has to keep them agreeing.
- **A separate `instance` table with a foreign key to the owner.** The fact leaves the user row, so every test that builds a user service has to seed a second table — for a guarantee the partial unique index and the two triggers already give.
- **A unique color per account.** A unique index on `color` caps the household at eight people and turns the ninth into a schema problem, and it makes picking a color a race rather than a preference - the swatch somebody was about to choose disappears under them. The picker marks a color another account already holds and lets it be chosen anyway, which is the whole of the constraint worth having.
- **A free color value rather than a palette token.** A hex string arrives as a background with no foreground to go with it, so the capital inside the circle has whatever contrast the person happened to land on, and one value cannot serve both themes. A closed set keeps the measured pairs in `frontend/src/app.css` and lets the API refuse everything else with an `enum` before a handler runs.
- **Soft-deleting users.** Nothing in the schema references a user except the recipes they wrote or edited and their sessions; reassigning the first and cascading the second beats a status column every query has to filter on.
- **Email as a login name or match key.** Nothing verifies a typed address, and a provider's address can be re-registered; `(iss, sub)` is the only link.
- **Restricting the contract routes further — to admins, or off entirely in production.** The contract is not admin-owned, and a logged-in member already knows the API from using the frontend; switching the docs page off takes it from the operator who has the most use for it instead of protecting it. A session of any role is the line.
- **zxcvbn's German and English language packs.** They add names, common words and Wikipedia vocabulary, and a megabyte gzipped on top of the 230 KB the leaked-password list costs; that list already catches most of what a household actually types.
- **Rating in the service.** No bundle cost, but a request per pause in typing and a Go port of zxcvbn that nobody maintains, against a standard-library-first service.
- **Refusing a weak password in the form.** A rule only the browser enforces is one API call away from not existing; a real refusal belongs in the service, next to the length rule.
- **Cropping on a canvas in the browser.** The upload would be a pre-cut bitmap: the service could not enforce a minimum, API and MCP clients would have to reimplement the crop, and a second pixel path would sit beside package image. The browser only reports the square it shows.
- **A two-step upload with a server-rendered preview.** A draft file to store and expire, and a second request before anything changes, for a preview the dialog already draws from the file in memory.
- **A crop library.** The math is a handful of pure functions in `frontend/src/lib/user/crop.ts` with their own tests, where a library would bring its own pointer handling and styling against the design system.
- **A throttle on setup-link redemption.** A 256-bit token cannot be guessed.
- **The token in the path.** It would land in proxy logs and Referer headers.

## Limits

- No account can use the admin reset on itself, and that falls out of the rank rule rather than being a case of its own: an admin's own row is an admin target, which only the owner may touch, and the owner's row is refused for every caller including the owner. Self-service is the profile page's own-password change, which verifies the current password and keeps the calling browser signed in - unless the account has none yet, in which case there is nothing to verify and the call sets one outright. The reset still ends every session of the account it is used on.
- The first start needs `REZEPTE_ADMIN_PASSWORD` while the `users` table is empty — `EnsureSuperadmin` returns `ErrAdminPasswordRequired` without it, and the service does not come up. `REZEPTE_ADMIN_USER` names that account and defaults to `admin`. Once an owner exists `EnsureSuperadmin` is a no-op and neither variable creates anything; a `users` table that holds rows but no owner is `ErrNoSuperadmin`, and the service refuses to start. Both values are taken after the substitution `--demo` performs (see [Demo mode](/features/demo-mode)), so a demo run without `REZEPTE_ADMIN_PASSWORD` supplies both credentials itself: nothing is required of the environment, and a `REZEPTE_ADMIN_USER` set there is discarded before anything reads it.
- Whoever knows a username can keep that account locked by failing on purpose, for at most 15 minutes after their last attempt — accepted, because the alternative keys need client addresses Rezepte cannot see behind a proxy. The owner is not exempt. Sessions already signed in are untouched, and `rezepte --reset-superadmin-password` does not go through the throttle.
- Because an attempt counts when it begins, several correct logins for one name started at the same moment count against each other, and past the fifth they are refused with `429`. People do not sign in that way; a script sharing one account across parallel workers would have to.
- The own-password change is not throttled. It takes a session of that account already, so it is no way in for someone who has none, and its argon2 evaluations sit in the same queue as every other.
- The throttle lives in memory: a restart forgets every lock, and it counts per process, which is all a single-binary SQLite deployment runs. A locked name is evicted only once 10,000 names are tracked and every one of them has used its free attempts — about 50,000 argon2 evaluations, four at a time.
- A flood of logins that keeps the argon2 queue full turns everybody else away with `503` for as long as it lasts: the cap trades availability for bounded memory, and a household member retries.
- There is no handover and no reset from inside the application. The bootstrap account is the owner for the life of the instance, no endpoint promotes a second one, and a forgotten owner password locks the instance out of its own administration until someone with shell access to the host runs `rezepte --reset-superadmin-password`. On a self-hosted household instance, giving up that account means giving up the instance.
